2. What actually is a cookie?

A cookie is a small text file — usually less than a kilobyte — that a website asks your browser to store on your device. That's it. It's not a program. It can't run code. It can't access other files on your phone or laptop. It can't see your photos, your messages, or your bank details.

What a cookie can do is remember a small piece of information and hand it back to the website the next time you visit. That's why you don't have to log in every time you open Gmail, and why an online shop remembers what's in your basket between visits.

On a tailoring website like ours, cookies do much less than on a shopping site — we don't have a customer login, we don't have a shopping cart, and we don't process payments on the site (you pay via M-Pesa on delivery, not online). Our cookies mostly do three things: keep the site running, tell us which service pages get read most often, and remember if you've already dismissed our cookie notice.

There is one small technicality worth knowing. A cookie is stored on your device, not on our servers. We can't go looking through your phone for it. The only way we ever "see" what's in a cookie is if your browser sends it back to us on your next visit — and only for the same domain that set it in the first place.

Cookies vs other similar technologies

Browsers also use things called local storage and session storage — which work like cookies but hold more data and don't get sent back to the server on every request. In this policy, we use the word "cookie" as shorthand for all of these tracking technologies, because from your point of view as a visitor, they do the same job.

3. How FitFixClothesAlterations.co.ke uses cookies

We use cookies for four specific purposes. Here's what each one is, in plain language, and what happens if you turn it off.

3.1 Essential cookies — the site won't work without them

Essential cookies are what let a website function at all. On FitFixClothesAlterations.co.ke, they do things like:

  • Keep your session open while you click through service pages.
  • Protect the contact form from spam bots (we use a lightweight anti-spam check).
  • Remember your cookie consent choice so you aren't asked again on every page.
  • Ensure the WhatsApp widget and contact buttons load correctly.

These cookies don't collect any personal information. They don't know your name, your email, or where you're from. They can't be switched off individually — without them, the site would break. You can still block them in your browser, but some features may stop working.

3.2 Performance cookies — how we know which pages people actually read

Performance cookies tell us things like: which service pages are visited most often, how long people spend on each page, which buttons they click, and where they land from (Google search, a WhatsApp link, a direct visit). We use Google Analytics 4 for this — it's the industry standard, used by millions of websites.

All data from Google Analytics is aggregated and anonymised by the time we see it. We don't get a report saying "David from Runda read the cargo pant page at 3:47pm." We get a report saying "the cargo pant page had 340 visits this month, average time on page 2 minutes 15 seconds, mostly from mobile devices in Nairobi." That's the level of detail we work with.

Performance cookies help us decide which services to write more detail about, which pages to improve, and whether our content is reaching the right people in Nairobi. If you turn them off, the site still works exactly the same for you — we just lose the anonymous data point.

3.3 Functionality cookies — remembering your preferences

Functionality cookies remember choices you make on the site so you don't have to make them again. On our website, that's mostly:

  • Remembering whether you've already accepted or declined cookies.
  • Remembering your preferred language if we ever add a Kiswahili version of the site.
  • Remembering the last service page you viewed, in case you want to return to it.

These are lightweight and short-lived. They're about convenience, not tracking. If you turn them off, you'll just be asked the same questions again on each visit.

3.4 What we do NOT do with cookies

To be clear and unambiguous — because we know this matters:

  • We do not sell cookie data to third parties.
  • We do not use cookies to build advertising profiles or retarget you with ads on Facebook or Google.
  • We do not track you across other websites.
  • We do not use cookies to collect your name, email address, phone number, or any other personally identifiable information.
  • We do not run behavioural advertising, pixel tracking, or any form of cross-site profiling.
  • We do not ask you to accept cookies before you can use the site — you can read every page without ever clicking "accept."

In short: our cookie use is minimal, boring, and functional. That's exactly how we want it to be.

6. How to control cookies — properly, browser by browser

You can block, delete, or limit cookies on your device at any time. You don't need to ask us, and you don't need to opt out of anything with FitFixClothesAlterations.co.ke directly — the control lives in your browser, not on our end. Here's exactly how to do it in the five browsers that cover about ninety-five percent of Nairobi's traffic.

6.1 Google Chrome (desktop)

Open Chrome and click the three-dot menu in the top-right corner. Go to Settings → Privacy and security → Third-party cookies. From there, you have three options:

  • Allow third-party cookies — everything works normally, analytics and all.
  • Block third-party cookies in Incognito — the middle option. Regular browsing is unaffected; only private windows block third-party tracking.
  • Block all third-party cookies — the strictest setting. Google Analytics won't fire on our site, but everything else still works.

To see and delete specific cookies, go to Settings → Privacy and security → Third-party cookies → See all site data and permissions. Type "fitfixclothesalterations" in the search box and you'll see every cookie our site has set on your device. You can delete them individually or all at once.

6.2 Google Chrome (Android)

Open Chrome, tap the three-dot menu, tap Settings → Site settings → Cookies. You can toggle "Allow cookies" off entirely, or manage individual sites by tapping All sites and searching for our domain. Deleting a site's cookies from this menu removes everything we've set.

6.3 Safari (iPhone, iPad, Mac)

Safari has some of the strongest cookie protections of any mainstream browser. On iOS, go to Settings → Safari → Advanced → Website Data. You'll see a list of every site that has stored data on your device. Search for "fitfixclothesalterations" and swipe left to delete.

To block cookies site-wide, go to Settings → Safari → Block All Cookies. Be warned — this also blocks essential cookies, which will break some features on our site (like the contact form and the cookie consent banner). It also breaks logins on most other websites, so most people don't leave it on permanently.

On Mac, go to Safari → Preferences → Privacy → Manage Website Data. Same interface, same control.

6.4 Mozilla Firefox

Firefox is the most privacy-friendly mainstream browser by default. Open Options (or Settings) → Privacy & Security. In the "Cookies and Site Data" section, you'll see two options:

  • Manage Exceptions — this is where you add our domain (fitfixclothesalterations.co.ke) to a block list. Blocking us here means our cookies won't be set at all.
  • Clear Data — this is where you wipe existing cookies. You can clear all cookies, or select specific ones by searching the list.

Firefox also has an "Enhanced Tracking Protection" mode (the shield icon in the address bar). Set to Strict, it blocks Google Analytics and most third-party tracking by default. If you're browsing with Strict on, our performance cookies aren't being set on your device — and the site still works fine for you.

6.5 Microsoft Edge

Open Edge and go to Settings → Cookies and site permissions → Manage and delete cookies and site data. You'll see a list of every site that has stored cookies, sorted by how recently they were accessed. Search for our domain and delete from there.

Edge also has a built-in setting called "Block third-party cookies" which you can turn on under the same menu. Doing so will block Google Analytics on our site — everything else continues to function.

6.6 What happens if you block our cookies entirely

Nothing breaks catastrophically. If you block all cookies on FitFixClothesAlterations.co.ke:

  • Every service page still loads normally.
  • The contact form still works — you can send us a message.
  • The WhatsApp button still opens your WhatsApp chat with David.
  • The cookie consent banner will reappear on every page load because we can't remember your choice.
  • We won't see your visit in Google Analytics, which means we lose one data point but nothing else changes for you.

In other words — you lose nothing important, and we lose nothing important. Block away if that's your preference.

7. Do Not Track signals and Global Privacy Control

Some browsers can send a "Do Not Track" (DNT) header with every request, signalling to websites that you don't want to be tracked. The problem is that DNT was never widely adopted and no major browser enforces it by default. Most websites — ours included — can see the header but choose to ignore it, because there's no legal agreement on what it should mean.

We've decided to be different. If your browser sends a DNT: 1 signal, we treat it as an opt-out from our performance cookies. Google Analytics won't fire on your visit. You'll still get the essential and functional cookies (because those are needed for the site to work), but no analytics.

To check whether DNT is enabled on your browser, visit whatismybrowser.com/detect/is-do-not-track-enabled. If it shows "Do Not Track is enabled," you're already opted out of our analytics. No further action needed.

We also respect the newer Global Privacy Control (GPC) signal where your browser supports it. Same logic: if you send it, we honour it.

8. Your rights under Kenya's Data Protection Act, 2019

Kenya's Data Protection Act (the DPA) came into force in 2019 and gives every Kenyan resident specific rights over their personal data. Because cookies can contain what the Act calls "personal data" — even if it's just a unique identifier — our cookie use sits inside the DPA's scope. Here's what that means for you, in plain language.

8.1 The right to be informed

You have the right to know what personal data we collect, why we collect it, and how we use it. This page is part of how we comply with that right. So is our Privacy Policy, which covers the data we collect when you contact us by WhatsApp, email, or the contact form.

8.2 The right of access

You can ask us to confirm whether we hold any personal data about you, and if so, what it is. For cookies, this usually means nothing — most people who visit FitFixClothesAlterations.co.ke leave behind only an anonymous analytics cookie. But if you've contacted us through the WhatsApp button or the contact form, we hold the details you provided (name, phone, garment description). You're entitled to a copy.

To make an access request, email davidochieng2345678@gmail.com with the subject line "DPA Access Request." We respond within 30 days, as required by law.

8.3 The right to correction

If any personal data we hold about you is wrong, incomplete, or misleading, you can ask us to correct it. Most commonly this comes up when a client's address changes between pickups, or when a WhatsApp number is updated. Just message us and we'll fix it — usually within a day.

8.4 The right to deletion (the "right to be forgotten")

You can ask us to delete any personal data we hold about you. For clients, that means your name, phone number, email, garment notes, and any WhatsApp conversation history we've kept for reference. We normally retain alteration records for 12 months (see our Privacy Policy for why), and we'll delete them sooner if you ask.

To make a deletion request, email davidochieng2345678@gmail.com with the subject line "DPA Deletion Request." We confirm the deletion within 30 days — usually within a week.

8.5 The right to object

You can object to how we process your personal data. For cookies, this is as simple as turning them off in your browser (section 6 above). For anything else, email us and we'll discuss it honestly. If we can't accommodate the objection for a specific legal or operational reason, we'll explain why.

8.6 The right to data portability

You have the right to receive any personal data you've given us in a structured, commonly used format — typically a PDF or CSV. If you want a copy of your alteration history or your contact details, just ask.

8.7 The right to lodge a complaint

If you believe we've mishandled your personal data, you have the right to complain. First, email us directly — most issues are resolved in a single reply, because small businesses answer to their owners. If you're not satisfied, you can escalate to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) in Kenya: odpc.go.ke. The ODPC accepts complaints in English and Swahili.

9. Our cookie consent mechanism

When you first visit FitFixClothesAlterations.co.ke, you'll see a small banner at the bottom of the screen asking whether you accept cookies. It has two buttons — one to accept, one to decline — and a link to this page. There's no default that assumes consent, and there's no requirement to click "accept" before you can use the site.

Here's exactly what happens when you click each button.

  • Accept: We set the cookie_consent cookie, Google Analytics fires on your visit, and the banner doesn't reappear for 12 months.

You can change your choice at any time by clearing the cookie_consent cookie from your browser (see section 6 above) and reloading the page. The banner will come back, and you can pick differently.

If your browser is set to send a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal, we skip the banner entirely — the signal is treated as a permanent "decline," and you'll never see the consent request.
