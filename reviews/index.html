How Reviews Work at FitFix

Reviews are one of the few things on the internet that a client can use to decide whether a business is worth trusting. That makes them valuable, and it also makes them something we take seriously enough to run a strict policy around. The policy is short, it is the same for every client, and it is the reason most of the reviews we receive end up published rather than sitting in a WhatsApp thread.

The FitFix review policy

We publish reviews on this page and on our Google Business Profile only when three conditions are met.

  • We have completed the job the client is reviewing.
  • The client has agreed to have their words published under at least a first name, and their estate where they are comfortable sharing it.
  • We can trace the review back to a Google review, to a WhatsApp thread, or to an in-person message that we can show on request.

If any of those three conditions is not met, the review stays unpublished. That includes anonymous reviews we cannot trace, positive reviews we cannot verify, and reviews of jobs we did not do.

What we do not do

  • We do not offer discounts, credit or any other incentive in exchange for a review.
  • We do not ask clients to say anything specific in a review or to phrase it a particular way.
  • We do not edit the substance of a review when publishing it, even if the review is critical.
  • We do not delete negative reviews, and we do not ask clients to take them down.
  • We do not buy reviews on any platform, and we do not run paid review campaigns.
  • We do not publish reviews from friends, family or anyone with a personal stake in the business.

Offering a reward for a review is against Google's own review policy, it undermines the whole purpose of reviews, and it would make every review on this page worthless to you as a decision-maker. So we do not do it, and if you ever see a claim that we do, it is false.

Why We Publish Fewer Reviews Than Other Businesses

FitFix has served thousands of Nairobi clients over the years, across every service from a KES 300 trouser hem to a KES 25,000 wedding dress restoration. If we chased reviews the way most businesses do, we could publish hundreds of them and fill a page ten times longer than this one. We do not, for one simple reason. A long list of reviews is only valuable to you if you can trust them, and you cannot trust reviews that were gathered under pressure, incentives or marketing strategy. A short list of verifiable reviews is worth more to both of us than a long list of quotes we cannot stand behind.

Most of our Nairobi clients simply do not leave reviews unless they are asked, and many prefer to keep their business private — especially clients in the diplomatic and expatriate community, clients in sensitive professions, and clients who value their privacy in a small city. We respect that. When a client does leave a review, we ask for their permission to publish it here, and if they say no we leave it on Google and move on.

The result is that this page grows slowly. A handful of reviews per quarter, sometimes fewer. That is the honest number, and we would rather have it be honest than padded.

What a FitFix Review Typically Covers

Because reviews are left by clients in their own words, they naturally vary in what they cover. In practice, the points that come up most often in reviews we receive are worth knowing about, because they are the same points a new client would want to ask about before booking.

Fit quality is the most common point. Clients describe how the alteration sat on the wearer, whether it looked natural, whether the seam line was visible, and whether the fit held across a full day of wear. For bridal and formal wear, reviews often mention the fittings — how many there were, how the schedule ran, and whether the dress or suit was ready in time for the event.

Pickup and delivery is the second most common point. Clients describe whether the rider arrived on the scheduled window, whether the item was handled carefully, and whether the delivery back happened when it was promised. For bridal and leather goods, they also mention whether the item came back in a protective garment bag and whether the finish was preserved.

Price and transparency is the third common point. Clients describe whether the price they were quoted on WhatsApp was the price they paid, whether there were any extra charges on delivery, and whether the payment on delivery worked without issue.

Communication is the fourth common point. Clients describe whether David responded within the hour, whether the assessment was honest about what could and could not be done, and whether they were told the truth about a garment that was past saving rather than being charged for a repair that would not hold.

How We Handle Negative Reviews

Every business receives a negative review at some point. How the business handles it is more important than whether it happens. Our policy is short and we follow it every time.

What we do when a negative review is left

We do not delete negative reviews. We do not ask clients to take them down. We do not respond defensively in public. We do not offer the client money to remove the review, and we do not offer the client a discount on a future job in exchange for the review being taken down. Those are all common practices in the industry and every one of them undermines the trust you place in a review page.

Where a negative review identifies a genuine problem with our work, we contact the client privately, offer to put the problem right at no cost, and confirm the resolution on the same public review thread so anyone reading the review later can see how it was handled. Where the negative review is mistaken or unfair, we respond once, factually, and leave the review in place. Where we were wrong, we say so.

Why We Do It This Way

A negative review is one of the few signals a client can use to gauge whether the business they are considering is honest. If a business's reviews are all five stars, either the business has never had a problem, or the problems have been removed and the honest reviews deleted. The first is unlikely after years of trading. The second is common, and it is exactly the sort of behaviour that makes review pages useless to the clients who read them.

We would rather have a page with a mix of reviews, including the occasional critical one, than a page that looks too clean to be real. And we would rather a client who had a bad experience come back and give us a chance to fix it than quietly leave a review we cannot address. Every negative review we have ever received has been handled the same way, and it is the policy going forward.
