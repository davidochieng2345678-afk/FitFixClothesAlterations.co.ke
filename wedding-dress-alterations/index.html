Why a Wedding Dress Is Not Just Another Alteration

A wedding dress is the single most demanding garment any tailor will ever work on. It has to fit perfectly on one specific day, to one specific person, with no margin for error and no second chance. It is usually the most expensive garment the bride has ever owned. It is often made from the most delicate fabric in the wardrobe — silk, chiffon, crepe, tulle, satin — with layers of lining, interlining, boning, lace, beading and, in many cases, family history stitched into every seam. And it has to be finished to a fixed date that cannot move.

That combination is why a wedding dress is not the sort of garment that gets handed to a general alterations shop. It requires a craftsman who understands the internal structure of a bridal gown, who can open a seam without damaging the lace or beading sitting over it, who knows the difference between a hem that is one centimetre too long and one that is one centimetre too short, and who will not force a fit that the fabric cannot take. That is what we have built the bridal side of the workshop around, and it is why most of our bridal bookings come from brides who were referred by a previous bride.

The second thing that makes a wedding dress different is the timing. Most wedding dresses in Nairobi arrive six to twelve weeks before the wedding date, and the alterations have to fit into the gap between arrival and the wedding day. Some brides have longer, some have almost none. A programme that runs six to eight weeks ahead of the wedding gives enough time for two fittings and a final adjustment. A programme that runs four weeks ahead is possible but compressed. A programme that runs two weeks ahead can only handle simple work. Getting the timing right at the start is what makes the rest of the programme work.

The third thing is the number of people involved. A wedding dress alteration is rarely just about the bride. The mother of the bride, the maid of honour, the bridesmaids, the flower girl, sometimes the groom's mother and sisters. Those garments are usually altered by the same workshop on the same booking so that everything sits correctly together on the day, and the whole bridal party comes back on one delivery. That is why we offer the bridal party package as a single line item rather than pricing each member separately.

What We Do on a Wedding Dress

Hemming measured to your wedding shoe

Hemming is the single most important alteration on a wedding dress because it sets the line of the gown on the day. Hemming only starts from KES 6,000. See bridal alterations for the full bridal service.

Bodice rebuilds and corset installation

Bodice rebuilds start from KES 14,000 and cover the bust, waist, back and internal structure. Corset installation starts from KES 12,000 and covers a full corset with boning, a lace-up back where required and a matching inner lining. Both are done with the lace, beading and embroidery removed and resecured by hand where necessary so the original decoration sits correctly over the new seam.

Bustle construction

Bustle construction starts from KES 4,500 and adds two to five concealed hooks or ribbons depending on the weight and length of the train. The bustle is fitted and tested at the second fitting so the bride and her maid of honour know exactly how to lift the train at the reception and how to fix it if it comes loose.

Lining replacement and structural work

Lining replacement starts from KES 9,000 and is done where the interior lining has worn, has gone soft, or needs to be reseated to accommodate a bodice rebuild or corset. We use matching fabric weight and finish so the lining sits correctly under the outer gown and does not show at the neckline, the sleeve or the back.

Lace, beading and embroidery preservation

Where a gown has heavy lace, beading or embroidery, the decorative layer is removed carefully at the seam line, the underlying seam is opened and reshaped, and the decoration is re-applied by hand so that the pattern continues correctly across the new seam. This is the most time-consuming part of most bridal alterations and is the reason a basic alteration on a beaded gown takes longer than a basic alteration on a plain satin gown.

Full gown restoration

Full restoration starts from KES 25,000 and covers a gown that has been stored for years, a vintage or heirloom gown being brought back into service, or a gown that needs substantial work across multiple areas — lining, corsetry, hem, straps, decoration and structure — as one programme. Restoration is priced on photo and on inspection because the work varies significantly from gown to gown.

Why Wedding Dress Alterations Matter More in Nairobi

Nairobi is not an easy climate for a wedding dress. The city sits at around 1,700 metres with a long dry season, a short rainy season, strong daytime sun, cool mornings and warm middays. A bride will usually be wearing the gown for eight to fourteen hours on the wedding day, moving between a morning ceremony, a photo shoot in the open air and a reception that may run late into the evening. The gown takes a genuine beating on that single day, and the alteration has to be built to handle all of it.

The first thing Nairobi weather does is swell and relax fabric across the day. A silk, chiffon or crepe gown that fits perfectly in the cool morning can feel tighter in the afternoon when the bride's body is warm and the fabric has taken the humidity. That is why we always advise a bodice fit that is snug at the first fitting but not tight, and why we leave enough depth in the bodice to allow a small adjustment if the bride's measurements change between fittings. A gown fitted too tightly on a cool Nairobi morning will be uncomfortable and unreliable by the midafternoon.

The second thing Nairobi weather does is stress the hem and the train. Outdoor photography on grass, on gravel or in gardens picks up moisture in the hem, and the train picks up fine grit. That moisture and grit work into the hem and the train structure faster than most brides expect, and if the hem is not finished to a proper depth with a clean edge, the hem will show dirt along the fold by the end of the day. We always recommend a hem depth that lets the bride lift the gown slightly during outdoor photography so the hem edge stays clean, and a hem edge finish that holds its shape through a full day of wear.

The third thing Nairobi weddings demand is the bustle. Kenyan weddings almost always include an outdoor photography session, and almost always move to a reception indoors or under a tent. That is a full change of environment for the gown, and the train has to lift cleanly and stay lifted for hours. A bustle that is only tested while standing still will often fail on the dance floor, which is why we test every bustle with the bride and the maid of honour at the second fitting, and why we always install more points than look necessary — three points on a short train, four or five on a full-length cathedral train.

Finally, Nairobi weddings often run late. The ceremony may start at 10am with a photography session that runs to 1pm, followed by lunch at 3pm and a reception that starts at 5pm and finishes at 11pm or midnight. That is a fourteen-hour day for the gown. A wedding dress that is altered correctly will hold its shape and its fit across all fourteen hours. A dress that is only altered to look right in a fitting room will start to move and to gap within the first few hours. We build every bridal alteration around the fourteen-hour day, not around the fitting room.

Rule of thumb for brides: book your wedding dress alterations the moment the gown arrives in Nairobi, not the week before the wedding. The difference between a comfortable bridal programme and a compressed one is almost always time, and time is what protects the alteration. A bridal programme that runs eight weeks ahead will always produce a better result than a programme that runs two weeks ahead.

Can you preserve lace, beading and embroidery during the alteration?

Yes. Preserving lace, beading and embroidery is the single most important part of wedding dress alteration. Every gown is opened along the least visible seams, the lace and beading are removed and resecured by hand where necessary, and the alteration is closed so that the original decoration sits correctly over the new seam. A bustle is a way of lifting the train of the wedding dress up and securing it at the back of the gown so that the bride can walk and dance at the reception without the train dragging on the floor.
