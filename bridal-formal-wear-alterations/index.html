Who I do this for

Most people who message me fall into one of three groups

Read the one that sounds like you. If none of them do, still message me — I've probably handled your situation before.

The bride whose dress arrived "close enough"

You ordered online, or bought off the rack, or inherited your mother's gown. It fits everywhere except the hem, or the straps, or the bust. You need somebody who has worked with lace, tulle, satin and beading before — and who won't ruin the fabric experimenting. That's most of my work.

The groom who wants it actually tailored

You bought a suit for the wedding and it's fine, but not right. Sleeves a touch long. Waist slightly loose. You want it sharp in the photos. Send me the suit — I'll shorten the sleeves properly, take in the waist, and have it back to you inside two days.

The bride planning for a whole party

You've got bridesmaids, two mothers, a flower girl, and maybe a ring bearer. Every dress needs something different. This is the kind of job I take on personally — I collect the whole batch from one address, alter them together, and deliver them back as a matching set.

Ask me anything

The questions I actually get asked, answered honestly

Fourteen years of bridal tailoring, and the same ten or so questions come up every week. I've put my honest answers below. If yours isn't here, WhatsApp me — I reply personally, not a receptionist.

Cost

"How much does it cost to alter a wedding dress in Nairobi?"

It depends on the fabric and the amount of work. Hemming on a simple satin gown starts at KSh 3,500. If the gown has lace overlay, beading, or a train, the hem alone can take four hours. Full body slimming — taking in the bodice and reshaping the side seams — starts at KSh 4,500. Bustle and train lifting, KSh 2,500. Strap adjustments from KSh 1,500. I'll give you a firm number on WhatsApp once I see a photo, and I won't move it after.

Timing

"How long before the wedding should I get the dress altered?"

Three to four months if you can. That gives room for a first fitting, a second fitting, and a final check three weeks out. But I know life doesn't always work that way. If you're two weeks out, message me — I've finished full bridal gown resizes in ten days. If you're three days out, message me anyway. I'll tell you honestly whether I can help.

Fabric

"My dress is silk. Can you alter it without ruining it?"

Yes — but slowly. Silk marks permanently if the needle or the iron is too aggressive. I hand-stitch silk hems and every seam gets pressed through a muslin cloth with low heat. Same for velvet and any fabric with a nap. If I look at a dress and think an alteration carries real risk, I'll say so upfront instead of charging you and hoping for the best.

Beading

"Can you alter a dress that has beading or lace on it?"

This is my speciality. Beaded and lace gowns are the ones most tailors refuse, because you can't just run them under a machine. I remove the beads along the seam by hand, alter the fabric underneath, then reattach every bead individually. It's slow work. On a heavily beaded bodice, reattaching can take a full day. But it is the only way to get the fit right without destroying the design.

Urgent

"My wedding is in four days — is that too late?"

Not always. I keep two slots a week open for exactly this. If the dress just needs a hem and a strap adjustment, I can do it in a day. If it needs a full bodice rebuild, four days is tight but not impossible. WhatsApp me the photo and the deadline. I'll give you a straight yes or no in five minutes — no dragging it out.

Fit

"My dress is too small. Can you make it bigger?"

Sometimes. Most quality gowns have 1 to 2 centimetres of seam allowance built in, which lets me let out the sides a bit. If there's no allowance left, the answer depends on the design — I can sometimes add a matching panel of lace or fabric that blends into the existing pattern. If I can't do it well, I'll tell you. I won't take a job that I know will look wrong when it's finished.

Groom

"Can you tailor the groom's suit and groomsmen's suits too?"

Yes, and I'd rather do it than have you take them somewhere else. Matching suits altered at the same time end up looking more consistent. I collect the whole set from one address, alter them together, and deliver them back together. Groom suit tailoring starts at KSh 3,500; groomsmen suits are the same rate, and I discount the group if there are five or more.

Pickup

"Do you come to my house, or do I have to bring the dress in?"

I come to you. Free, anywhere in Nairobi — Runda, Kilimani, Karen, Kasarani, Ongata Rongai, Kitengela, Utawala, all of it. You send me a photo first, I quote you, then I send somebody to collect. When the dress is done I bring it back and you try it on in your own home. If anything needs a final tweak, I take it back with me and return it. No charge for any of that.

How a bridal job runs, start to finish

From WhatsApp to the aisle — the actual timeline

Most brides I work with go through five moments. Here they are, in order, with honest notes about what happens at each.

The photo comes in

You message me on WhatsApp

Send a photo — front, back, and a close-up of whatever isn't working. Add your wedding date and the estate you live in. I'll reply with a firm price and a pickup window. No call centre, no receptionist. You're talking to me directly.

Within 24 hours

I collect the dress

Somebody comes to your door and collects the gown. Free, anywhere in Nairobi. If your dress lives at a bridal boutique or at your mother's house, tell me — I'll collect it from there instead.

1–2 days

First fitting — done properly

I pin the gown to your measurements. This is the point where I decide whether to shorten the hem from the bottom or lift it from the waist — they're two very different surgeries, and the right one depends entirely on the fabric and the design. Sometimes I ask you to come in and try the dress on mid-alteration so I can check the fit before I commit any stitches.

3–5 days later

Alteration & second fitting

The dress is altered and finished. I call you to schedule the second fitting — this happens at your home, or at the workshop if you'd rather come in. If the fit is right, we're done. If a strap needs another centimetre, or the bustle sits slightly off, we make the correction and I bring it back to you. Free.

Final delivery

The dress comes home — finished

I deliver the gown back to your door, folded on a padded hanger, in a breathable garment bag. If your wedding is a few weeks away, keep it in the bag and don't touch it. Any last-minute issues the week of the wedding, message me — I always keep a slot clear for bridal emergencies.
