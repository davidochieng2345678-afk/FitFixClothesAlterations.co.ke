What Actually Happens on the Bench

Curtain alteration looks simple from the outside and is genuinely fiddly on the bench. The reason is that a curtain is a large, heavy, two-layer construction — a face fabric and a lining — and the two layers have to be shortened, narrowed or re-headed together without either of them puckering, drooping or going slack. A curtain hem that is not set correctly will hang unevenly, break at the pleats and pull at the heading tape. That is why the workshop process is slower than most people expect, and why we do the whole thing in-house rather than sending curtains out.

The first job on the bench is measurement. The curtain is laid flat on the long table, the existing hem depth and lining attachment are checked, and the new length is marked on both the face and the lining. Where the curtain is lined, the lining is shortened slightly less than the face so the curtain does not sit flat against the window. Where the curtain has a blackout coating, the coating on the inside of the face has to be protected during the process — cutting or overheating the coating is what turns a working blackout curtain into a failed one.

The second job is opening the seam. On a lined curtain, this means separating the lining from the face along the bottom hem, and on a curtain that is being narrowed, along the side seams. Opening the seam cleanly without damaging the fabric is the step that most distinguishes a proper curtain alteration from a rushed one. A heat-sealed seam or a glued seam is what most curtain shops use to speed things up, and it is also what fails first when the curtain is next cleaned or exposed to a Nairobi dry season.

The third job is the re-stitch. The new hem is machine-stitched along the fold, and where the heading tape has to be reseated, the tape is opened, the curtain is refolded to the correct pleat depth, and the tape is restitched at the original pitch so the pleats match the original set. On an eyelet curtain, the top fold is reset so the rings sit at the correct height and the eyelets align. Where the curtain has pencil pleat tape, the gathering cords are re-fed and the pleats are checked by hand so the curtain opens to the correct width.

The fourth job is the press and hang. Every altered curtain is steamed and pressed flat before it leaves the workshop, and where the client requests it we hang the curtains back on the rail ourselves on the return delivery to confirm the drop. If the drop is not correct on the first try — which occasionally happens because the curtain was measured against a slightly different rail height than the client's actual rail — we take the curtain back and re-do the hem at no cost. That is the reason we insist on getting the rail height right on the collection photo.

What Curtain Alterations Cover

Eight services make up most of the curtain work we do in Nairobi. Each one is priced on photo, per panel, and confirmed before pickup.

1. Curtain hem shortening

The single most common curtain alteration in Nairobi. Curtains puddle on the floor when they are too long, and puddling curtains pick up dust, get caught on shoes and look tired within a season. Hem shortening starts from KES 1,500 per panel and covers opening the existing hem, marking the new length, shortening the lining slightly less than the face, re-stitching the hem at the original depth and pressing flat. Drop is measured against the client's actual rail height, not a standard drop.

2. Curtain width narrowing

Curtains that are too wide do not close properly because the pleats spread too far apart, and they look bunched against the wall. Width narrowing starts from KES 1,800 per panel and covers opening the side seams, removing the correct width from each side so the curtain stays centred on the window, re-stitching the side seam, and re-working the heading tape so the pleat pitch matches the new width. Where the heading is eyelet, the rings are repositioned at the new width and the top fold is reset.

3. Re-heading on pencil pleat, pinch pleat and eyelet

Re-heading starts from KES 1,800 per panel and covers replacing or reseating the heading tape so the pleats sit evenly. Pencil pleat tape has to be reseated with the gathering cords re-fed and the pleat pitch matched to the original set. Pinch pleat headings have to be re-formed by hand at the correct depth. Eyelet headings have to be re-punched or repositioned at the correct spacing so the curtain hangs evenly on the rod.

4. Standard relining

Relining starts from KES 2,500 per panel and covers removing a failed lining, cutting and fitting a new lining in matching or upgraded fabric, and re-stitching the lining to the face along the bottom hem and the side seams. Standard linings are cotton, poly-cotton or a light thermal weave. The correct lining weight depends on the face fabric and the amount of light the client wants to block, and we confirm the correct lining on photo before starting.

5. Blackout curtain repair and relining

Blackout curtain repair starts from KES 3,200 per panel. Blackout curtains fail when the coating on the back of the face fabric cracks or peels, when the curtain has been dry cleaned with a solvent that broke down the coating, or when the curtain has been stored folded for a long period and the fold lines have cracked. The correct fix is to reline with a new blackout lining. Where the face fabric itself has failed and cannot be relined cleanly, we fit a separate blackout liner on its own rail behind the existing curtain at a lower cost than a full rebuild.

6. Thermal and insulated relining

Thermal relining starts from KES 3,800 per panel and covers fitting a thermal or insulated lining behind the existing curtain. Thermal linings reduce heat gain through the window in the hot season and reduce heat loss at night, and they also soften outside noise. The correct thermal lining for a Nairobi home or office depends on the window aspect, the face fabric and whether the client also wants blackout. We confirm the correct option on photo before starting.

7. Curtain stain and damage repair

Stain and damage repair starts from KES 1,500 per panel and covers patching, re-stitching split side seams, repairing torn headings, and concealing small marks with matched fabric where the original fabric can be re-used from a hidden section. Where a stain or damage is beyond repair on the visible part of the curtain, we tell you honestly on photo and discuss options such as replacing the affected panel or moving the curtain to a less visible window.

8. Full curtain panel rebuilds

A full panel rebuild starts from KES 9,000 per panel and covers opening the entire curtain, replacing both the lining and the heading tape, resetting the side seams, reworking the hem and pressing the finished curtain flat. A full rebuild is appropriate for high-quality curtains that have been stored for years, curtains from a previous property that need to be re-sized for a new window, and curtains whose face fabric is sound but whose internal construction has failed. The rebuild keeps the face fabric and replaces everything else.

Curtain Types We Handle

Every curtain we alter is handled on the same bench as the tailoring work, and the correct approach depends on the heading type and the construction. Below are the types we handle most often and what the alteration involves for each.

Pencil pleat curtains

Pencil pleat is the most common heading style in Nairobi homes. The heading uses a woven tape with three gathering cords that pull the fabric into fine vertical pleats. Shortening a pencil pleat curtain means opening the bottom hem, marking the new length, and re-stitching the hem without disturbing the heading. Narrowing a pencil pleat curtain means opening the side seams, removing width evenly from each side, and reseating the heading tape at the correct pitch. The pleats must be checked by hand after reseating to make sure the curtain opens to the correct width when the cords are pulled.

Eyelet and ring-top curtains

Eyelet curtains hang on a rod through metal rings punched into the top hem. Eyelet is the simplest heading to shorten because the drop is set by the rings and the bottom hem is the only thing that moves. Narrowing an eyelet curtain is more involved because the rings have to be repositioned at the correct spacing, the old eyelet holes have to be closed, and the top fold has to be reset so the curtain falls evenly. We punch the new eyelet holes with a matched die so the rings sit as cleanly as the originals.

Pinch pleat, triple pleat and goblet pleat curtains

Pinch pleat and triple pleat headings are formed by hand at fixed intervals along the top of the curtain, usually with a stiffener inside. These headings cannot be shortened from the top, so any length reduction is done at the bottom hem. Narrowing is done carefully at the sides so the hand-formed pleats at the top stay at the correct pitch relative to the new width. Where the pleats have lost their shape from age or dry cleaning, we re-form them by hand as part of the alteration.

Tab top and rod pocket curtains

Tab top curtains hang from fabric loops at the top of the panel. Rod pocket curtains hang from a sewn pocket that the rod slides through. Both are simple headings that are shortened at the bottom hem, and both can be narrowed at the side seams without disturbing the top. Where a client wants to convert a rod pocket curtain to a tab top or an eyelet heading, we can rework the top as part of the same job.

Wave and ripple fold curtains

Wave and ripple fold headings produce an even wave across the full width of the curtain, usually running on a specific wave track or rod. These are the most delicate headings to alter because the wave geometry has to be preserved at the exact pitch or the curtain will not sit correctly on the track. Wave curtains are shortened at the bottom and, where necessary, narrowed from the sides. We confirm the correct pitch on photo before starting.

Lined, unlined, blackout and thermal curtains

Unlined curtains are the simplest to alter because there is only one layer. Lined curtains have a separate face and lining that have to be shortened or narrowed together. Blackout curtains have a coated face or a blackout lining and need care during stitching so the coating is not damaged. Thermal curtains have a heavier inner lining that adds weight to the hem and requires reinforced stitching. Each type is priced differently because the work is different, and the price is confirmed on photo.

Voile, sheer and lightweight curtains

Voile and sheer curtains are the most delicate curtains we handle. The fabric is light, the hem is often a rolled hem, and the stitching has to be fine enough not to show through. Sheer curtain shortening starts from a higher price than a standard curtain because the hem has to be rolled and stitched at a much finer gauge and the fabric is easier to damage with a misplaced pin. We handle voile and sheer curtains regularly for Nairobi homes with double-rail windows, where the sheer sits behind a heavier face curtain.

Why Curtains Fail in Nairobi

Nairobi's climate and the way Nairobi homes are built put a specific set of stresses on curtains. Understanding them is the reason the workshop takes a slightly different approach to curtain alterations than it does to garment alterations, and it is the reason a curtain alteration done well here lasts several years longer than the same alteration done without accounting for the local conditions.

UV damage to the face fabric

Nairobi sits at around 1,700 metres above sea level with strong daytime sun for most of the year. A curtain hanging against a west-facing or north-facing window in a Nairobi home takes more UV across the year than the same curtain in a lower-altitude city. UV fades the face fabric unevenly, it dries out the fibres and it cracks the blackout coating on the back of the face fabric. That is why curtains in Nairobi often fail first on the side of the window that faces the afternoon sun. When we alter a curtain that has failed on one side, we check whether the opposite side is starting to fade, and we tell you honestly what to expect.

Dust along the bottom hem and the leading edge

The dry season in Nairobi throws fine grit off murram roads, construction sites and dry verges. Curtains that reach the floor pick up dust along the bottom hem and along the leading edge where they slide against each other. That dust works into the hem stitching and the seam allowance, and over a few seasons it dulls the fabric, darkens the hem line and accelerates wear. Shortening a curtain that puddles on the floor solves the problem in one step, because the amount of fabric touching the floor is reduced and the hem line is moved up to a cleaner part of the curtain. That is one of the reasons puddle-length curtains in Nairobi rarely last as long as curtains hemmed to just clear the floor.

Humidity during the long rains

Nairobi's long rains push humidity up across the city for several weeks each year. Curtains stored against the window during that period can pick up a faint musty smell on the lining, and the lining fabric softens. A lining that has gone soft loses its body, which is why lined curtains that go through two or three long-rain seasons without being aired will start to hang unevenly. Relining a curtain that has reached this stage is the correct fix and the result is a curtain that behaves like new. Lining work starts from KES 2,500 per panel.

Dry cleaning and heat damage

Curtains that are dry cleaned with a solvent that is too aggressive for the face fabric will lose their blackout coating, their pleat memory and, in some cases, their surface finish. Curtains that are pressed too hot will scorch and crack the coating. Blackout curtains in particular are sensitive to both. When a curtain arrives at the workshop with a failed blackout coating, the first thing we check is whether the failure is from dry cleaning or from age, because that determines whether relining will hold or whether the face fabric needs a different approach.

Curtains that were sized for a different window

A huge share of curtain alteration work in Nairobi comes from people who have moved house and brought the curtains from their old home. A curtain that hung perfectly in a three-metre window is too long and too narrow in a two-and-a-half-metre window. A curtain from a flat with a low ceiling is too short for a house with a higher one. A curtain that worked in a north-facing bedroom is too heavy for a west-facing lounge. Every one of those cases is a straightforward curtain alteration — shortening, narrowing, re-heading or relining — and every one of them is cheaper than buying a new set of curtains.

Rule of thumb for Nairobi curtains: a lined curtain that is shortened to just clear the floor, re-headed at the correct pitch, and lined with a quality lining will last three to five times longer than the same curtain left at puddle length with a heat-sealed hem. The difference is the alteration, not the fabric.

Can you make curtains longer instead of shortening them?

Sometimes. A curtain can be lengthened where the original hem has enough depth left in it or where a matching fabric can be sourced to add a band at the bottom or behind a heading. We do not offer a full dry cleaning service.
