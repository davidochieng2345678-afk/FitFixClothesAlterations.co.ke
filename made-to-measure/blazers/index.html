Why Off-the-Rack Blazers Never Fit Nairobi Professionals

The blazer is the single hardest garment to fit off the rack, and the one where a wrong fit is most obvious. Most working professionals in Nairobi who wear a blazer to the office have at least one that sits wrong somewhere, and they know it.

  • Shoulders that sit an inch or two wider or narrower than your actual shoulder line.
  • Chest that fits but pulls at the button, or fits at the button but balloons at the chest.
  • Sleeves that are too long and hide the shirt cuff, or too short and show two inches of cuff.
  • Jacket length that sits above the hip or below the knuckle, when it should sit between.
  • Waist that looks straight when it should taper, or pinches when it should flow.
  • Collar gap at the back of the neck that no amount of tailoring can fully fix.

The problem is not your body. Blazers are cut for a factory average that almost nobody matches, and once the shoulder and the collar are wrong, alteration cannot fully correct them. Made-to-measure starts from your measurements so the shoulder, the collar and the chest are correct from the first stitch. That is why a made-to-measure blazer looks different from an altered off-the-rack one, even when both are technically the same colour.

Every Blazer Detail Is Chosen by You

A made-to-measure blazer is not just about the fit. Every construction detail is decided at your consultation, from the shoulder line to the way the lining is finished. Below are the choices we walk you through.

Shoulder construction

Soft shoulder, natural shoulder or structured shoulder. A soft shoulder is lightly padded and sits low, which suits unstructured blazers and Nairobi's warmer months. A natural shoulder follows your shoulder line with minimal padding. A structured shoulder is firmer, sits slightly higher and gives a sharper, more formal line. Getting the shoulder construction right is what makes a blazer look like it belongs on your body rather than on a mannequin.

Lapel

Notch lapel, peak lapel and shawl lapel. Notch is the standard for business blazers and looks correct in every setting. Peak is more formal and adds a sharper line to the chest. Shawl is the most formal and is usually reserved for evening wear. Notch lapel width is also a choice: narrower for a modern look, wider for a traditional one. All three are available at every lapel width.

Button front

Single-breasted one, two or three-button fronts, or double-breasted. Two-button single-breasted is the most common and the most versatile. One-button is more modern and suits a shorter, sharper jacket. Three-button is more traditional and works for taller clients. Double-breasted is formal and adds presence, and can be cut with four, six or eight buttons. All versions are available.

Lining

Full lining, half lining or unlined. Full lining is the standard and gives the cleanest interior finish. Half lining is cooler and lighter, best for warm months and lighter fabrics. Unlined blazers have no internal lining, which is the coolest option but only works on structured fabrics that hold their own shape. Lining colour can match the fabric or contrast deliberately. Patterns and monograms on the inner pocket are available on request.

Pockets

Flap pockets, patch pockets, welt pockets, jetted pockets, ticket pocket, breast pocket with or without a pocket square flap. Flap pockets are the standard for business blazers. Patch pockets are more casual and suit unstructured blazers. Welt and jetted pockets are sharper and more formal. The number and placement of pockets is confirmed at your consultation.

Vents

Single vent, double vent or no vent. Double vents are the most common on a business blazer and give the jacket a cleaner line when your hands are in your pockets. Single vent is more traditional and works for shorter jackets. No vent is very rare and mostly used on formal jackets. All three are available and the choice is confirmed at consultation.

Length

Blazer length is measured from the collar down to the hem. The correct length sits between the top of the hip and the knuckle of the thumb. We measure your length and cut the jacket to the correct point for your body. Getting the length right is what keeps the button stance at the correct place and stops the blazer from looking either cropped or oversized.

Sleeves

Sleeve length is measured to the wrist bone so the shirt cuff shows by the correct amount, usually about half an inch. Sleeve width is cut to your arm so the sleeve does not balloon at the forearm. Working cuffs with functional buttonholes are available on request. Sleeve lining colour can be matched to the jacket or contrasted.

Button choice

Buttons are chosen at consultation. Options include horn, plastic in matched or contrasting colour, metal and mother of pearl. Buttons can be tonal for a subtle finish or contrasting for a more deliberate look. Back collar and sleeve buttons can be matched or contrasted with the front buttons. All versions available.

Men's and women's blazers are cut from different base patterns and measured differently. Both are handled on the same bench, with the same fabric range and the same fit guarantee.

The shoulder slope is one of the most overlooked measurements on a men's blazer and it is the reason an off-the-rack jacket will often show a gap at the back of the neck or a bulge at the front. Getting the shoulder slope correct is what makes a blazer sit cleanly on the body from the first fitting.

Women's made-to-measure blazers

Women's blazers are cut to a different base pattern that accounts for the bust, the waist and the hip, with the correct amount of ease at the bust and the correct button stance. The measurements we take include the bust, the underbust, the natural waist, the hip, the shoulder, the shoulder slope, the back length and the sleeve. The button stance on a woman's blazer sits at a different point than on a man's, because the bust changes where the jacket closes cleanly. That is the single most common reason a woman's off-the-rack blazer does not fit properly, and it is why made-to-measure is the correct solution.

Women's made-to-measure blazers start from KSh 8,000. Available in classic single-breasted, soft unstructured, longer-line and cropped versions. What a Made-to-Measure Blazer Fixes That Alteration Cannot

Alteration can improve a blazer that is already close to your size. Made-to-measure fixes the things alteration cannot reach, because it starts from your measurements rather than from a size chart.

  • Shoulder width. Cut to your actual shoulder line. Not an inch wide, not an inch narrow. The most important measurement on any blazer.
  • Shoulder slope. Cut to the exact slope of your shoulders. Stops the back collar gap and the front chest bulge that off-the-rack blazers always show.
  • Chest and waist balance. Chest cut to your chest, waist tapered to your waist, hem cut to sit cleanly at the hip. No pull at the button, no balloon at the chest.
  • Button stance. Placed at the correct point for your body. Higher for a longer line, lower for a shorter one. Correct for your waist position.
  • Jacket length. Cut to the correct point between the hip and the knuckle. Not cropped, not oversized.
  • Sleeve length and width. Sleeve set to the correct length for your shirt cuff to show properly. Sleeve width cut to your arm so the sleeve does not balloon at the forearm.
  • Lapel. Cut to the correct width and shape for your chest and shoulder.
  • Lining and interior. Colour, pocket style, monogram and interior finish all chosen by you.
