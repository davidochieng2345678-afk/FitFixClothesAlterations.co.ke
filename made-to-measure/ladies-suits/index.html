Why Off-the-Rack Ladies Suits Never Fit Nairobi Professionals

Women's suits are the hardest category in off-the-rack clothing. A men's blazer can be adjusted with a few alterations. A woman's blazer cannot, because the bust changes the entire geometry of the jacket. That is the reason most working women in Nairobi have at least one blazer that closes awkwardly, or a pair of suit trousers that gap at the waist while fitting the hips.

  • Blazers that fit the bust but gape at the button, or close at the button but pull at the bust.
  • Suit trousers that fit the hips but need a belt pulled tight at the waist.
  • Shift dresses that fit the waist but are too wide at the shoulder or too short in the body.
  • Pencil skirts that fit the hip but pinch or ride up when you walk or sit.
  • Jackets that sit too low or too high at the button stance because the pattern was built for a different torso.
  • Sleeve length cut for a standard arm that does not match yours.

The problem is not your body. Factory sizing for women's suits is built around a standard block that almost nobody matches. When a suit is cut to your bust, waist, hip and shoulder as separate measurements, the fit stops being a compromise. That is why made-to-measure ladies suits in Nairobi look different from an altered off-the-rack suit, even when both are technically the same colour and fabric. The difference is in the pattern underneath.

Every Ladies Suit Detail Is Chosen by You

Every made-to-measure ladies suit in Nairobi is built around the details you choose. Below are the choices we walk you through at your consultation.

Button stance on the blazer

The button stance is the position of the closing button on the blazer and it is the single most important detail on a women's blazer. On a men's blazer, the button sits at the natural waist. On a women's blazer, the correct position depends on the bust, and getting it right is what makes the jacket close cleanly without gaping at the chest. We measure the bust and the underbust at consultation and place the button stance at the correct point for your body. You see the result at your first fitting and we adjust if needed.

Lapel

Notch, peak and shawl lapels are all available at every width. Notch is the standard for a working blazer. Peak is more formal. Shawl is the most formal and is usually reserved for evening wear. Lapel width is cut to the correct proportion for your shoulder and bust.

Fit shape

Shaped, semi-fitted or straight. A shaped blazer curves in at the waist for a feminine silhouette. A semi-fitted blazer skims the body without pinching. A straight blazer falls clean from the shoulder to the hem, which is the more traditional choice for a formal office. All three are available and the choice is confirmed at consultation.

Trouser rise and cut

Rise options are mid, high and low. High rise is often the better choice for a woman's trouser because it sits at the natural waist and does not gap. Cut options are straight leg, wide leg, tapered and cigarette. Waistband options include belt loops, side adjusters, and a hidden elastic insert for comfort. All options are available.

Trouser front

Flat front is the modern default. Single pleat and double pleat give more room at the hip and are useful for a fuller thigh or for trousers worn with a longer blazer. Both are cut to the same standard.

Trouser closure

Side zip with a hook and bar closure is the traditional women's trouser closure and it gives a cleaner front. Fly front with a button or hook closure is the modern alternative and gives a slightly sharper look. Both are available.

Shift dress details

Back zip or side zip, lined or unlined, with or without pockets, walking vent or back slit, sleeve or sleeveless. Neckline options include round, boat, V-neck and square. All confirmed at consultation. We do not cut sleeveless on a shift dress without discussing whether you want the option to wear a blazer over it, because the shoulder line sits differently in each case.

Pencil skirt details

Length options are knee length, just above the knee and midi. Side or back zip, walking vent at the back, lined or unlined, with or without pockets. The fit is cut with the correct amount of ease so the skirt does not pull when you walk or sit. Available in wool, wool blend, wool crepe, cotton twill and stretch fabrics.

Lining and interior

Full, half or unlined blazers. Shift dresses and pencil skirts are lined by default in wool and dress fabrics, and unlined in summer fabrics. Lining colour can match the outer fabric or contrast deliberately. Inner pocket monogram is available on request.

Buttons

Horn, plastic, metal and mother of pearl, in matched or contrasting colours. Button choice is confirmed at consultation. Working cuffs with functional buttonholes are available on the blazer sleeves on request.

The Measurements That Make a Ladies Suit Fit Properly

Every made-to-measure ladies suit in Nairobi starts with a full set of measurements taken at your home or office. Below are the measurements we take and why each one matters.

★

Bust and Underbust

The most important measurements on a women's blazer. They set the button stance, the shape of the chest panel and the amount of ease at the bust. Getting these right is why made-to-measure fixes the gaping button problem.

★

Natural Waist

Measured at the narrowest point of the torso. Sets the waist shaping on the blazer and the rise on the trouser or skirt. Getting the waist measurement correct is what stops the suit from pulling or gaping.

★

High Hip and Full Hip

Measured separately. The high hip sits a few centimetres below the natural waist and the full hip sits at the widest point. Both are needed because women's trousers and skirts have to fit correctly at both points.

★

Shoulder Width and Slope

The shoulder measurement decides how the blazer sits across the top of the body. The shoulder slope decides the angle. Both are taken separately and both are cut into the pattern. This is what makes the shoulder sit cleanly on a real body.

★

Sleeve Length and Width

Sleeve length is measured to the wrist bone so the correct amount of shirt or blouse cuff shows. Sleeve width is measured at the upper arm and the forearm so the sleeve does not balloon or pinch.

★

Back Length and Front Length

The back length sets the total jacket length. The front length is often different because of the bust, and getting both correct is what keeps the jacket sitting level rather than riding up at the front.

Linen and Linen Blends

The coolest option for Nairobi's dry season and for the coast. Breathes, moves and looks correct in a smart casual setting. Blends reduce the natural creasing of pure linen.

What a Made-to-Measure Ladies Suit Fixes That Alteration Cannot

Alteration can improve a ladies suit that is already close to your size. Made-to-measure fixes the things alteration cannot reach, because it starts from your measurements rather than from a size chart.

  • Button stance on the blazer. Placed at the correct point for your bust so the jacket closes cleanly instead of gaping. The single most important fix on a women's suit.
  • Bust shaping. Cut to your bust and underbust as separate measurements. No pulling at the button, no slack at the chest.
  • Shoulder width and slope. Cut to your actual shoulder line. Stops the collar gap at the back and the pulling at the front.
  • Waist and hip balance on trousers. Cut to your natural waist and your full hip as separate measurements. No belt pulled tight to hold the trouser up, no gaping at the back.
  • Trouser rise. Cut at the correct position so the trouser sits at the natural waist and does not ride down when you stand up.
  • Shift dress body length. Cut to your actual torso length so the waist sits in the correct place and the dress does not look too short in the body or too long.
  • Pencil skirt ease. Cut with the correct amount of ease so the skirt does not pull when you walk or sit. Walking vent set at the correct length.
  • Sleeve length and width. Cut to your arm so the sleeve does not balloon at the upper arm or hide the cuff of a shirt or blouse.
