Make your first message count

The WhatsApp message that gets a firm quote in one reply

Most people send "hi, do you alter suits?" and wait. That first message doesn't get you a price — David still needs four answers. Here's the message that does.

Copy this template and fill in the blanks

Hi David,

Garment: [e.g. men's navy wool suit jacket]
Issue: [e.g. sleeves are about 3cm too long, and I want the waist taken in slightly]
Location: [e.g. Kilimani]
Deadline: [e.g. before Friday 12th, or "no rush"]
Photo: [attach one front photo, one back photo, and a close-up of the sleeve]

Why this works: In one message, David knows what you have, what you need, where you are and when you need it back. He can usually reply with a firm price on the first message. If the garment is unusual — leather, beaded, vintage — add one extra close-up of the fabric and he'll tell you honestly what's possible.

If you're not sure what's wrong with the garment

Hi David,

Garment: [type]
Problem: [in plain words — "it feels baggy", "the sleeves drag", "it sits too low"]
What I'd like: [even if you're not sure — "I want it slimmer", "not sure"]
Location: [estate]
Photo: [attach 2-3 photos from different angles]

You don't need to know tailoring terms. "It feels baggy" is enough for David to figure out what needs doing. He'll tell you what he sees and what he recommends, then you decide.

After your message lands

From the moment you hit send, to the moment your garment comes home

You've sent the WhatsApp. Here's exactly what happens next.

01

Your message arrives on David's phone

Every WhatsApp goes to David's personal phone, not a shared inbox or a business account with a team behind it. During working hours he sees messages as they arrive. If he's mid-alteration there may be a short delay — otherwise you hear back within about five minutes.

02

David replies with a firm quote

If the alteration is straightforward, you get a firm number, a turnaround time and a pickup window immediately. If he needs more information — a fabric close-up, a back photo — he asks in the same reply. If the job isn't advisable — the fabric is too tired, the shoulder is too narrow — he'll tell you that straight.

03

You confirm and we schedule a pickup

If the quote works for you, tell him a time. Morning, lunchtime, evening, Saturday — whatever fits your day. A rider arrives at your home or office at that window, collects the garment and confirms the details in person. You don't pay anything at pickup.

04

The work happens at the Industrial Area workshop

Your garment comes back to the bench. Simple jobs finish the same day. Waist take-ins and slimming take a day or two. Shoulder work, leather work and full restyles take longer — and David will have told you that at the quote stage.

05

You get a message when it's ready

David messages you when the alteration is finished — not the rider — so you know exactly what was done and if anything worth flagging came up. Then you agree a delivery window.

06

Delivered back, paid for, and guaranteed

Same rider brings the garment back to your door. Try it on. Pay via M-Pesa, cash or bank transfer. If anything isn't right — the sleeve half a centimetre short, the waist a touch tight — hand it back to the rider and David will re-do it under the workmanship guarantee. No arguments, no re-quote.

A short note from David

What it's actually like to message me — and why you'll never get a script

I check WhatsApp myself. Not my team, not a receptionist — me. Same for the emails. That's not a marketing line, it's just how I set up the business. When you message FitFix, you're talking to the person who will actually alter your clothes. I think that matters, because it means I can answer things honestly instead of routing you through someone who has to guess.

There are three things I always tell people when they ask me how to get the best out of the conversation. First, send photos. Not one — send two or three. A front shot, a back shot, and a close-up of whatever isn't working. It takes you thirty seconds and saves us five messages. Second, tell me your estate and your deadline. Most jobs get scheduled faster when I know those two things upfront. Third, don't worry about using the right words. You don't need to know what a "flat-felled seam" is. "The sleeve hangs past my hand" is enough. I'll work out the rest.

If a job isn't worth doing, I'll say so. If a garment has been cut too far to be altered, I'll say so. If a rush fee is going to apply, I'll say that too, in the first reply, before you commit. Nobody should ever be surprised by a bill after the fact, and nobody should feel pressured into an alteration that isn't going to help.

Fourteen years of this trade has taught me that most people aren't looking for the cheapest tailor in Nairobi. They're looking for one who tells them the truth. That's what you'll get from me.
