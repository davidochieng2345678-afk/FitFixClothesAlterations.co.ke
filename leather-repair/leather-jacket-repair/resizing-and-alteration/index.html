What Makes Leather Jacket Resizing Different

Altering a leather jacket is not the same as altering a fabric jacket. Every stitch made in leather leaves a permanent mark. That changes the whole approach to resizing. On a fabric jacket, a seamstress can unpick a seam, move it by a centimetre, and stitch it back with no visible trace. On a leather jacket, moving that same seam leaves a line of needle holes along the old stitch line that will never fully close.

That is why leather jacket resizing has to be planned around the existing seams. Where the adjustment is small, we work through the original stitch lines so no new holes are made. Where the adjustment is larger, we plan the new seam to sit as close to the original as possible and refinish the panel so any visible marks blend into the leather finish. Where the jacket has no seam allowance at all, we tell you honestly that it cannot be let out without adding a panel, and that is not the repair most people want.

Why Leather Jacket Fits Fail in Nairobi

Nairobi is not a harsh desert and it is not a tropical rainforest, but the combination of the two, plus the way jackets are bought here, produces fit problems that most owners cannot fix themselves.

Second Hand and Mitumba Fit

Nairobi has one of the deepest second hand leather markets in East Africa. A well chosen mitumba jacket can be better hide than anything new at three times the price. The catch is that the jacket was made for another body. Shoulders sit wide, sleeves hang long, the waist is loose and the overall length may not suit the new wearer. That is where resizing comes in, and it is one of the most common jobs we handle on second hand jackets.

Weight Change

A leather jacket bought five years ago may no longer fit the wearer today. Weight gain tightens the chest and shoulders. Weight loss leaves the waist and sides loose. Because leather does not stretch or shrink like fabric, the change has to be handled by moving seams rather than washing the garment to size. We adjust the jacket to fit the current body so it stays correct as long as the wearer's weight is stable.

Shopping in the Wrong Fit

Online purchases and imported jackets often fit differently from the labels. A jacket marked as the wearer's size may sit boxy on the shoulders or hang long in the body because it was cut for a different body shape. Rather than sending the jacket back, most owners bring it to us to adjust it to their actual body shape.

Style Change

Sometimes the jacket fits but the style no longer matches what the wearer wants. A boxy biker jacket that was right three years ago may now be wanted as a fitted jacket. A long trench that was correct in one era may now be wanted shorter. Both are achievable with a resize, and both are common jobs at FitFix.

Previous Alterations That Moved the Wrong Seams

A significant portion of our resize work involves undoing previous alterations. Seams moved too far and leaving a visible line of holes. Sleeves shortened at the wrong end so the shoulder seam now sits wrong. Waist taken in so tight that the jacket pulls at the zip. Correcting these costs more than doing the job properly the first time, which is exactly why we do not cut corners.

Leather Does Not Behave Like Fabric

Every seam in leather leaves a permanent mark. Every stitch hole stays visible for the life of the jacket. That is the reason most tailors will not touch a leather jacket and the reason most owners assume resizing is impossible. On a well built jacket with reasonable seam allowance, resizing is entirely possible, and that is the work we have been doing for fourteen years.
