What Makes Leather Jacket Repair Different

A leather jacket is one of the few garments people genuinely keep for decades. It outlives trends and often carries memory. When a jacket fails it is almost never the leather itself that has given up first. It is the zip, the lining, the cuffs, the collar, the stitching or the surface finish. All of those are repairable, and repairing them properly costs far less than replacing the jacket.

Leather does not behave like fabric. A zip that has failed cannot be replaced the way a fabric zip is replaced. A lining that has torn cannot be cut the same way a fabric lining is cut. A redye has to go into the hide rather than sit on top. A resized jacket has to account for the fact that every stitch leaves a permanent mark in the leather. Getting any of those wrong turns a repair into damage that cannot be undone.

Why Leather Jackets Fail Faster in Nairobi

Nairobi is not a harsh desert and it is not a tropical rainforest, but the combination of the two destroys leather jackets faster than most owners realise.

Strong UV at Altitude

Nairobi sits at altitude with strong midday sun for most of the year. Ultraviolet light breaks down the oils inside leather and dries out the waxes that hold the surface finish together. Jackets worn in the sun for a season fade from the shoulders down. Brown jackets go patchy, black jackets go grey, and the surface finish starts to crack at the flex points across the back and shoulders.

Repeated Wetting and Drying

The April, May, November and December rains soak jackets through, then the sun dries them fast. This cycle pulls the oils out of the leather, hardens the hide and starts the cracking that eventually peels. Repeated wetting also weakens the stitching and the zip tape, which is why the zip on a rain exposed jacket often fails before the leather itself.

Dry Season Air

Nairobi's dry months pull moisture out of stored leather. A jacket hanging in a dry cupboard through a dry season loses its oils and goes stiff. Once the leather is stiff, it cracks at the flex points. Conditioning the jacket twice a year is what keeps it supple enough to move without splitting.

Improper Storage

Leather needs air. A jacket folded and pressed under other clothes creases permanently at the fold. A jacket stored in a plastic bag traps moisture against the leather and grows mould. A jacket left on a wire hanger distorts the shoulders. The damage from bad storage shows up months later as creases that crack, shoulder distortion and uneven fade.

Sweat Inside the Lining

The lining of a leather jacket takes the full salt and moisture load of the body every time it is worn. Salt is abrasive against lining fabric. Over years of wear it eats through the lining at the armpits and back seam. That is why linings usually fail before the outer leather and why the armpits are the first place to check on a jacket that has been worn for several seasons.

Bad Previous Repairs

A significant portion of our jacket work involves undoing previous repairs. Zips that were replaced with hardware in the wrong finish. Linings that were reattached with the wrong thread. Crack repair done with surface paint that cracked again within months. Resizing done without allowing for the permanent needle marks leather takes. Correcting these costs more than doing the job properly the first time, which is exactly why we do not cut corners.
