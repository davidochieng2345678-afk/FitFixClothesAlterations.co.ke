What Makes Cuff and Collar Repair Different

The cuffs and collar of a leather jacket are the two points that touch skin, sweat and friction every time the jacket is worn. The cuffs move with the hands, rub against the wrists, catch on tables and doors and take the load of the arms swinging all day. The collar takes contact with the neck and the hair, absorbs sweat on the inside and takes the sun on the outside. That is why cuffs and collars wear out faster than any other part of a leather jacket, and why they are usually the first thing to look tired on a jacket that is still structurally sound.

Repairing cuffs and collars is different from repairing a panel or a seam. The cuffs and collar sit on the edge of the jacket, so any repair has to look right from the outside and feel right from the inside. A cuff that is rebuilt in the wrong leather shows against the sleeve. A collar that is replaced in the wrong ribbing tension distorts how the jacket sits across the shoulders. A ribbed knit cuff that is fitted in the wrong gauge bunches against the wrist within a week. Getting any of that wrong turns a small repair into a visible one.

Why Leather Jacket Cuffs and Collars Fail Faster in Nairobi

Nairobi is not a harsh desert and it is not a tropical rainforest, but the combination of the two destroys jacket cuffs and collars faster than most owners realise.

Strong UV at Altitude

Nairobi sits at altitude with strong midday sun for most of the year. Ultraviolet light dries out the oils inside the cuff and collar leather, and dries the waxes that hold the finish together. Cuffs and collars sit at the edges of the jacket, so they see more direct sun than the panels. That is why the cuff edge often fades and cracks before the body of the sleeve does. Once the leather at the cuff is dry it splits along the fold with every flex.

Sweat and Salt

The collar takes contact with the neck across every wear. Sweat and salt work into the lining side of the collar and the leather edge itself. Salt is abrasive against leather. Over years of wear it dries the collar fold out, cracks the edge and leaves a permanent dark rim inside the collar. Washing the collar edge with a leather specific cleaner and conditioning twice a year is what extends the life of the collar fold.

Repeated Wetting and Drying

The April, May, November and December rains soak jacket collars through, then the sun dries them fast. This cycle pulls the oils out of the collar leather and hardens the fold. Once the fold is hard, it cracks along the same line every time the collar moves. Collars that have been rained on multiple times are the most common crack repair we see.

Friction Against Wrists, Tables and Doors

The cuff of a leather jacket takes constant friction. Hands brush against the cuff when reaching into pockets. Cuffs rub against tables when sitting at a desk. Cuffs catch on doors, car door frames and gates. That friction is what wears the cuff edge through to the lining. Even the best leather cuff will need rebuilding after years of this kind of contact, and rebuilding it in matching leather is what keeps the jacket usable.

Worn Ribbed Knit

Ribbed knit cuffs and collars on bomber jackets and some biker jackets lose their tension with age. The ribbing stretches out of shape and no longer holds the wrist or the neck. Once the knit has gone soft, it is not recoverable and needs to be replaced with fresh ribbing in the correct gauge and colour. This is one of the most common jobs we do on bomber jackets.

Bad Previous Repairs

A significant portion of our cuff and collar work involves undoing previous repairs. Cuffs rebuilt in the wrong leather shade. Collars stitched with thread that is too heavy so the edge sits proud of the jacket. Ribbed knit replaced in the wrong gauge so the cuff bunches at the wrist. Correcting these costs more than doing the job properly the first time, which is exactly why we do not cut corners.
