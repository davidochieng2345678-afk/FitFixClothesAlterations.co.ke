What Makes Leather Jacket Zip Replacement Different

A zip on a leather jacket is not the same as a zip on a fabric jacket. The leather face cannot be drilled, glued or stitched through without leaving a permanent mark. Every stitch hole made in leather stays visible forever. That means a zip replacement on a leather jacket has to go back through the original seam line of the jacket, following the stitching that was already there, so the leather face is not touched. That is the difference between a zip that is invisible after replacement and one that leaves a line of fresh holes along the placket.

The zip weight also matters. A standard fashion zip is too light for a leather jacket that has to hold its weight on the shoulders through daily wear. We fit zips in the correct gauge for the jacket construction, whether it is a lightweight bomber, a mid weight blazer or a heavy biker jacket with an asymmetric front zip. Matching the slider, the pull, the tape width and the finish to the original hardware is what makes the replacement look factory.

Why Leather Jacket Zips Fail Faster in Nairobi

Nairobi is not a harsh desert and it is not a tropical rainforest, but the combination of the two destroys leather jacket zips faster than most owners realise.

Strong UV at Altitude

Nairobi sits at altitude with strong midday sun for most of the year. Ultraviolet light dries out the wax and oil inside the zip tape and cracks the surface finish on the leather around the placket. Once the tape is dry, the teeth start to separate from it and the slider loses grip. That is why a jacket worn through a Nairobi dry season often loses its zip before the leather itself shows any wear.

Repeated Wetting and Drying

The April, May, November and December rains soak jackets through, then the sun dries them fast. This cycle pulls the oils out of the leather and warps the zip tape. On a metal zip it also starts the corrosion that dulls the brass and nickel finishes. Repeated wetting and slow drying is the leading cause of zips that stick halfway and sliders that stop locking.

Dust and Fine Grit

Nairobi's dry months produce fine dust that works its way into every slider and between every tooth. Inside a zip that dust acts like sandpaper, grinding the slider against the teeth every time the jacket is closed. A slider that is carrying dust loses its grip within weeks even when the rest of the zip is sound.

Storage Without Movement

Jackets stored for months without being opened or closed let the zip take a set at the fold. When the jacket is finally worn, the slider has to force past the set and the tape often splits along the fold. Opening and closing the zip every few weeks during storage is what keeps the tape supple and the slider free.

Bad Previous Repairs

A significant portion of our zip work involves undoing previous repairs. Zips that were replaced with the wrong tape colour. Sliders that were swapped in mismatched finishes. Stitching that cut through the leather face rather than following the original seam line. Correcting these costs more than doing the job properly the first time, which is exactly why we do not cut corners.

Age of the Original Zip

Most leather jackets come with a zip that was fitted to the leather weight at the factory, and that zip has an end of life just like the jacket. When the tape has dried out and the slider has lost its tension, the correct repair is a full replacement rather than another slider swap. We will tell you honestly which of those two the jacket needs.
