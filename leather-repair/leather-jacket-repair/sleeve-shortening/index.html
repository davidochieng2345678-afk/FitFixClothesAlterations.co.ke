The Cuff Is What Makes This Job Difficult

Anyone can cut a sleeve shorter. The work is in what happens to the cuff. Leather jacket cuffs carry the shape, the weight and often the only piece of hardware or detail on the whole sleeve. Lose the cuff and the sleeve looks wrong from the outside even if the length is correct. That is why we plan every sleeve shortening around one question first: how do we keep the cuff.

There are three ways depending on the jacket. Shorten from the cuff end, reset the cuff to the new position and hide the old stitch line on the inside of the sleeve. Shorten from the shoulder end where the cuff cannot be moved cleanly, and rebuild the sleeve head so nothing shows on the outside. Or, where the cuff is a knit or ribbed cuff on a bomber, unstitch the knit, shorten the leather and reattach the original cuff or a fresh one in the same colour and tension. Most jackets fall into the first category. Biker jackets with zip cuffs and knit cuff bombers fall into the other two.

David Ochieng has shortened sleeves on hundreds of leather jackets in Nairobi since 2011, from basics to full-on zip cuff biker jackets. He will tell you in the first WhatsApp message which method applies to your jacket and whether the cuff can be kept.
