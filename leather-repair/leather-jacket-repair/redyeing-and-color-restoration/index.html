What Makes Leather Jacket Redyeing Different

Leather redyeing is not painting. Surface paint sits on top of the leather, cracks the first time the leather flexes and comes off with wear. A penetrating dye goes into the hide and moves with the leather, so the colour does not crack, peel or come off on clothes. That is the whole difference between a redye that lasts years and a coat of coloured polish that fails within months.

Redyeing a jacket is also different from redyeing a pair of shoes. A jacket has more surface area, more seams, more flex points and often more than one type of leather or finish on the same garment. The dye has to be applied evenly across panels of slightly different age, differently conditioned leather and, in mitumba cases, sometimes two different hides stitched together. Getting the process right means stripping the old finish, conditioning the leather so it absorbs evenly, mixing the dye to the correct shade, applying it in layers, sealing it and finishing it with a protective top coat that lets the leather breathe.

Why Leather Jackets Fade Faster in Nairobi

Nairobi is not a harsh desert and it is not a tropical rainforest, but the combination of the two destroys leather jacket colour faster than most owners realise.

Strong UV at Altitude

Nairobi sits at altitude with strong midday sun for most of the year. Ultraviolet light breaks down the pigments in the leather finish and bleaches the surface layer. Black jackets go grey from the shoulders down. Brown jackets lose their depth and go pale. Tan jackets go almost cream on the shoulders and sleeves. The fade starts subtly and by the time it is obvious the surface layer has already lost most of its pigment.

Repeated Wetting and Drying

The April, May, November and December rains soak jackets through, then the sun dries them fast. This cycle pulls the dye out of the leather and leaves water marks on the surface. Pale rings across the chest and shoulders are the most common water stain we see, especially on brown and tan jackets. Once the water mark has set into the leather it will not come out with polish alone and needs a redye.

Dry Season Air and Drying Leather

Nairobi's dry months pull moisture out of stored leather and dry the surface faster than the leather can reabsorb it. Dry leather does not hold pigment as well as well conditioned leather, so it fades faster under the same sun. Conditioning twice a year is the cheapest form of insurance against premature fade.

Dust and Fine Grit

Nairobi's dry months produce fine dust that acts like sandpaper on the leather surface. Every time the jacket brushes against a chair, a wall or a car seat that dust abrades the finish and dulls the colour. Regular brushing and conditioning keeps the finish intact for longer.

Bad Previous Redye

A significant portion of our redye work involves undoing previous redyes. Surface paint that cracked within months. Colour that came off on clothes. Dye applied unevenly so one panel is darker than another. Colour that does not match the original and looks applied rather than factory. Correcting these costs more than doing the job properly the first time, which is exactly why we do not cut corners.

Age of the Original Finish

Every leather finish has an end of life just like the leather itself. When the surface pigment has thinned to the point where the base leather is showing through, no amount of polish will bring it back. That is the point where a redye becomes the correct repair, and we will tell you honestly whether your jacket has reached it.
