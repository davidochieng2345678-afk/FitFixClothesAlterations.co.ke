What Makes Wedding Shoe Care Different

A wedding shoe has two jobs. It has to look correct in the photographs and it has to be comfortable for twelve hours on the day. That combination changes what the shoes need. A quick polish is not enough. The groom's Oxfords, the groomsmen's Derbies or loafers and the bridesmaids' heels all have to be cleaned, conditioned, colour matched and protected on the same schedule so the group looks consistent from the preparation photos to the last dance.

There is also a deadline. A wedding has a date and that date does not move. Cleaning, colour work, repair and protection each take a certain amount of time, and the schedule has to work backwards from the wedding day, not forwards from the workshop. That is why we ask for the date first and quote the turnaround second.

Why Wedding Shoes Need Special Care in Nairobi

Nairobi is not a harsh desert and it is not a tropical rainforest, but the combination of the two affects wedding shoes in specific ways that single pair care does not.

Shoes Are Bought Months Before the Wedding

Most couples buy wedding shoes two to four months before the date. Between the purchase and the wedding, the shoes sit in a box or a wardrobe through a dry season, a wet season or both. Leather dries out in storage. Colour settles. Patent goes cloudy. Suede flattens. By the time the wedding week arrives, the shoes look a season older than the day they were bought.

Strong UV at Altitude

Nairobi sits at altitude with strong midday sun for most of the year. If wedding shoes have been worn to any other event before the wedding, the sun will have faded the colour, particularly on brown and tan leathers. Groomsmen shoes worn to different events before the wedding fade at different rates, and the group looks uneven as a result.

Rain in the Wedding Season

Nairobi wedding season coincides with the April, May, November and December rains. Wedding venues range from indoor reception halls to outdoor gardens, and shoes need to handle any of them. A water resistant protector applied before the day is what keeps the leather safe from rain splash and mud on the way in.

Long Wear on the Day

A wedding day is not a normal wear day. Shoes are worn from morning preparation through to the reception and often into the night. That is twelve to sixteen hours on the foot. Leather that has not been conditioned will crack at the flex point by the end of the day. Groomsmen shoes in particular take a heavy load on the dance floor and on steps.

Group Consistency in Photographs

A wedding is one of the few events where the shoes of a whole group appear in the same photograph at the same time. Any mismatch in shade, finish or edge dressing shows in the images. That is why we match the group first and finish the group last, so the pairs come back looking like they came from the same shop on the same day.

Last Minute Emergencies

The week before a wedding is when most couples discover a problem. A scuff on the groom's shoes. A broken heel on a bridesmaid shoe. A buckle that has come loose. A pair that has been sitting in the box and turns out to need more than a polish. Our last minute emergency care is built for exactly this, with priority batching and a fixed turnaround that fits the remaining days before the date.
