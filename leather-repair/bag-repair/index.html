Why Bags Fail Faster Than Everything Else

Bags take more concentrated abuse than any other leather item. A laptop bag is loaded and unloaded twice a day, dropped onto car seats and set down on floors. A briefcase is carried by one handle in one hand for hours at a time. A backpack hangs off two straps that carry the entire weight of the contents against two anchor points. A duffel gets dragged through airports, bus parks and hotel lobbies by whichever strap the owner can grab first.

That load concentrates at exactly the same points every time. The handle root. The strap anchor. The zip end where the pull bends the tape. The gusset corner that folds every time the bag is closed. Those points take ten times the stress of any other part of the bag, and they wear through years before the rest of the leather even shows use.

That is why bag repair is rarely about the leather itself. It is about the anchor, the stitching, the hardware and the structural build inside the bag. Fix those properly and a good bag lives another five years. Fix them badly and the repair fails within weeks, at the same point, with more damage around it than before.
