What Makes Sneaker Cleaning Different From Shoe Polishing

A sneaker is not a leather dress shoe. A single sneaker can be leather on the upper, suede on the heel panel, mesh on the tongue, rubber on the midsole and canvas on the lining. Each of those materials wants a different product and a different technique. Suede needs a brush and a suede specific cleaner, never water or wax. Mesh needs a soft brush and a gentle foam. Leather can take a cream and a conditioner. The midsole is a rubber or foam compound that yellows, scratches and stains differently from every other part of the shoe. Getting any of those materials wrong leaves a mark that cannot be reversed.

Midsole whitening is the second thing people ask about. A white sneaker that has gone grey or yellow is not dirty. It is oxidised. Cleaning alone will not bring back bright white. The midsole needs a specific brightening routine, and on leather midsoles we can repaint back to a factory white that holds for months without cracking. That is the difference between a clean pair and a restored pair.

Why Sneakers Fail Faster in Nairobi

Nairobi is not a harsh desert and it is not a tropical rainforest, but the combination of the two destroys sneakers faster than most owners realise.

Strong UV at Altitude

Nairobi sits at altitude with strong midday sun for most of the year. Ultraviolet light breaks down the pigments in the sneaker upper and the rubber compound in the midsole. White midsoles oxidise to yellow within a single dry season. Coloured uppers fade and lose their depth. Leather sneaker uppers dry out and start to crack at the flex point across the toe.

Fine Dust That Acts Like Sandpaper

Nairobi's dry months produce fine dust that works its way into canvas weave, suede nap and mesh panels. Inside the material that dust acts like sandpaper, wearing the fibres every time the sneaker flexes. On suede the dust flattens the nap. On canvas it works into the weave and dulls the colour. On mesh it clogs the openings and reduces breathability.

Rain and Mud Splash

The April, May, November and December rains splash mud up the sides of sneakers and leave water marks on suede that set hard within a day. Once water has been absorbed into suede and dried without cleaning, the stain can set permanently. Getting the sneaker cleaned within a few days of a muddy walk is what prevents that stain from becoming permanent.

Sweat and Odour Build Up

Nairobi's warm afternoons and daily wear cause sweat to build up inside the sneaker. The lining, the insole and the footbed all hold moisture, and bacteria grow inside the shoe within days of regular wear. Simply washing the upper does not remove the smell. The interior needs a specific treatment with an anti microbial product to remove the source of the odour, not just mask it.

Bad DIY Cleaning

A significant portion of our sneaker work involves correcting damage from home cleaning. Washing machine cycles that shrink canvas and separate the sole from the upper. Bleach that eats through suede and leaves a yellow mark. Dish soap that strips the leather finish and leaves a dull dry surface. A stiff brush that scratches mesh and flattens nap. Correcting these costs more than doing the clean properly the first time, which is exactly why we do not cut corners.

Storage Without Stuffing

Sneakers stored without stuffing lose their shape, particularly at the toe box and the heel counter. Once the toe box has creased permanently, the crease becomes the crack. A simple rolled sock or a shoe tree keeps the sneaker shape and takes the load off the flex point between wears.
