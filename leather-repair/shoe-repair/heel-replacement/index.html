Heel Top Piece Versus Full Heel Replacement

The first question in any heel job is whether you need the top piece replaced or the whole heel rebuilt. This is the difference between a KES 600 job and a KES 1,500 job, and getting it right saves you money in the long run.

Heel Top Piece Replacement

The top piece is the thin layer of rubber or leather at the very bottom of the heel that actually touches the ground. It wears fastest because it takes the full impact of every step. When the top piece has worn through to the heel block but the block itself is still solid, only the top piece needs replacing. The old top piece is removed, the bottom of the block is levelled flat, a new top piece is cut to match the shape of the heel and attached with adhesive and nails or stitching. This is the cheapest heel repair and one of the highest value repairs in shoe care.

Full Heel Replacement

When the heel block itself has cracked, collapsed, come loose from the sole or worn down past the point where a new top piece will hold, the whole heel needs to be rebuilt. The old heel is removed, the heel seat is cleaned and repaired if necessary, a new heel block is built up in the correct material and to the correct height, shaped to match the shoe, and then the top piece is fitted over it. This is a much larger job than a top piece replacement and it is what protects the shoe from further damage.

How to Tell Which You Need

  • Top piece only: The heel block is still level and firm. Only the very bottom layer has worn through, usually on the outside back corner.
  • Full heel replacement: The heel block has cracked, is coming apart in layers, has come loose from the sole, has worn down at an angle or is soft under pressure.
  • Heel seat repair: The heel has come away from the sole entirely, or the sole around the heel seat has split. This requires the heel seat to be rebuilt before the new heel goes on.

If you are not sure, send a photo of the heel from the side, one from the back and one from underneath. David will tell you which category you fall into and quote the correct repair rather than the more expensive one.

Why Heels Wear Unevenly and What That Tells Us

Nearly every pair of shoes we see for heel replacement has worn unevenly. One heel is worn through and the other is barely touched. This is not a shoe fault. It is a walking pattern, and reading that pattern is how a good craftsman builds a heel that lasts.

Outer Back Corner Wear

This is the most common pattern in Nairobi. It happens when a person pushes off slightly outward at each step. The outside back corner of the heel takes almost the entire load and wears through first. When we replace the heel we thicken the outside corner slightly and use a harder top piece material on that side so the shoe wears evenly over the next cycle.

Inner Edge Wear

Less common, but seen in people whose feet roll slightly inward. The inside edge of the heel wears first and the shoe feels like it is twisting underfoot. We adjust the heel shaping so the load distributes across the whole base of the heel rather than concentrating on the inside edge.

Full Heel Flattening

When a person walks with a flat footfall, the entire heel wears evenly and flattens rather than wearing through on one corner. This pattern is easier to fix because the shoe is not unbalanced, but the heel needs to be rebuilt in the correct height rather than simply capped with a thicker top piece.

Toe Heavy Walkers

Some people barely touch their heels down at all. Their heels wear slowly but their forefoot area wears fast. When this pattern shows up during a heel job, we point it out because the shoe may need a resole sooner than expected. See shoe resoling in Nairobi for that conversation.

Heel Materials We Use and When We Use Them

The material of the heel top piece decides how long it lasts, how quiet it is underfoot and how it looks on the shoe. Matching the material to the shoe is a large part of what makes a heel replacement feel correct.

Rubber Heel Tops

Rubber heel tops are the most common choice for everyday leather shoes and boots. They grip well, they are quiet on hard surfaces, they wear reasonably and they are easy to replace again later. Rubber is the correct choice for work shoes, casual leather shoes, walking shoes and most men's leather shoes worn daily in Nairobi.

Leather Heel Tops

Leather top pieces are the traditional choice for welted dress shoes and Italian leather footwear. They look correct on an Oxford or a fine Derby, they quiet the walk, and they wear at a rate that lets the shoe rotate properly between wears. Leather wears faster than rubber, so leather tops suit shoes worn on rotation rather than every day.

Polyurethane Heel Tops

Polyurethane is harder wearing than plain rubber and quieter than some hard plastics. It grips well, resists abrasion and holds its shape. It is a good middle ground for casual shoes worn frequently but not daily.

TPU and Hard Plastic Heel Tops

TPU and hard plastic heel tops are hard wearing, quiet and long lasting. They are common on European shoes and are what we fit when a customer wants maximum life from a heel top without changing the look of the shoe.

Stacked Leather Heels

Stacked leather heels are built up layer by layer from thin pieces of leather, glued and pressed together then shaped by hand. This is what higher end welted shoes use for a proper period look. When a stacked leather heel collapses, we rebuild it in matching stacked leather rather than fitting a solid rubber block, so the shoe keeps its original character.

Women's Heel Materials

Women's heels include block heels, stiletto heels, kitten heels, wedge heels and stacked heels. Each needs a different material and a different attachment method. Block heels use rubber or polyurethane. Stilettos need a small, hard, sharp edged tip that keeps the profile slim. Kitten heels are often leather or plastic capped. Wedge heels are usually rubber or polyurethane. We match the material to what the shoe originally had so the look is preserved.

What Affects the Final Heel Repair Price

Five things move the final figure up or down from the starting price.

  • Heel type. A standard men's heel block is straightforward. A stacked leather heel, a stiletto heel or a kitten heel each need more hand work and more careful matching.
  • Heel material. Rubber and polyurethane are cheaper. Leather and stacked leather cost more because of the material and the extra shaping.
  • Condition of the heel seat. If the seat is sound, the job is straightforward. If the seat needs repair or the sole has split around the heel, additional work is added.
  • Shoe size and height. Taller heels and larger shoe sizes use more material and take more shaping time.
  • Additional work required. Resoling, stitching, colour restoration and polishing can be added to the same heel job if the shoe needs it. Each is priced separately and quoted before starting.

Practical Shoe Care That Protects Your Heels

  • Check the heel top every month. A top piece replacement at KES 600 is far cheaper than a full heel rebuild at KES 800 or a heel seat repair at KES 1,500.
  • Do not drag your feet when you walk. Dragging wears the heel top unevenly and shortens its life considerably.
  • Rotate your shoes. Never wear the same pair of leather shoes two days in a row. Twenty four hours of rest lets moisture escape and the heel block recover its shape.
  • Use shoe trees or paper. Cedar shoe trees absorb moisture and hold shape. Rolled newspaper works reasonably well if trees are not available.
  • Dry away from direct heat. Never dry wet leather shoes on a radiator, in an oven or in direct sun. Let them dry slowly at room temperature so the heel glue does not soften.
  • Wipe rain off promptly. When leather gets wet, blot it with a dry cloth and let it dry slowly. Do not rub, because rubbing pushes dirt into the grain.
  • Use a shoe horn. A shoe horn saves the heel counter from being crushed every time the shoe goes on.
  • Do not pull shoes off by standing on the opposite heel. This breaks down the heel block and the sole around the heel seat faster than anything else.
