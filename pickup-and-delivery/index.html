Why We Do Pickup Free Instead of Charging for It

Most workshops in Nairobi charge for pickup and delivery because it is a real cost. Fuel, rider time, a working phone line and the admin of tracking every item in and out all add up. We decided years ago that charging separately for it would only slow down the job, because the client who is debating between "send it in and pay a rider" and "walk it in myself on a Saturday" is a client who often just gives up and buys a new shirt. The cost of pickup is small when it is spread across a working day of collections in the same estates, and the benefit of removing the friction is that people actually send their items in to be repaired instead of letting them sit in the wardrobe.

That decision has built a specific kind of client base. A corporate client in Upper Hill who has three suits that need the jacket sleeves shortened will not send a junior staff member out for half a day to drop the suits at a workshop in another part of town. But they will leave the suits at reception with our rider's name written on a note, and they will pay by M-Pesa on delivery because the whole thing happens inside the building they work in. A parent in Karen with three school uniforms to alter will not drive across town with a boot full of shirts, but they will hand the uniforms to the gate security guard at 8am and get them back on Friday. A bride in Lavington with a wedding dress and four bridesmaid dresses will not want to move that much fabric around the city in a car, but she will schedule one free pickup and let us handle it all on one invoice.

Removing the pickup fee also lets us do the small jobs that other workshops do not want. A single belt that needs a buckle replaced, one wallet with a split fold, a pair of shoes that need a polish. Those jobs are not worth the ride on their own, but they are worth doing when a rider is already passing through Westlands or Kilimani that afternoon and can add them to the round. Our riders run scheduled routes through the major estates every working day, and small jobs slot into those routes at no extra cost to the client. That is why we can offer a free pickup on a KES 500 wallet repair and still be profitable, when most workshops would need to charge a KES 300 delivery fee just to make the job worthwhile.

Free pickup also means we see the item in person before we start. Most Nairobi workshops quote from a photo and then discover on inspection that the job is more complex than it looked, and either the client is charged more than the quoted price or the workshop quietly makes a loss. Our rider photographs the item on collection, we compare the collection photo to the client's original photo, and if the two match, the price does not change. If the collection photo shows a hidden problem, we call before starting the work and either revise the price with the client's agreement or return the item untouched and free of charge. That is the entire reason the free pickup is worth more than the price of a ride.

The one thing we ask in return is accurate photos and a nominated collection point. Two clear photos and a correct estate or building name take ten seconds to send and save us an hour of routing. Where the pickup point is a reception desk or a gate, tell us the receptionist's name or the gate number so the rider walks straight to the right place without a call. Small details on the client's side turn a good pickup into a same-day pickup, and same-day pickup is the difference between an item that gets fixed before the weekend and one that waits until Monday.

These clients do not have time to leave a workday for a tailor. They book a pickup that lands at reception during lunch or after hours, and they get the item back at the same desk a few days later. It is the same pattern whether it is a suit that needs the jacket sleeves shortened, three shirts that need slimming, or a pair of leather shoes that needs a resole. Three or four uniforms at a time, collected from the estate gate on Monday and returned on Friday. Wedding gowns, bridesmaid dresses, mother-of-the-bride outfits and groomsmen suits all move together on one booking, one pickup and one delivery. How We Keep Your Items Safe in Transit

A pickup is only as good as the care taken moving the item between your address and the workshop. Over the years we have built a set of habits that mean almost nothing gets damaged in transit, and the ones below are the ones that matter most.

Protective garment bags for formal and leather items

Any bridal gown, formal dress, suit jacket, leather jacket or any item made from a delicate fabric travels on a dedicated hanger in a protective garment bag. That bag stays with the item through the workshop and is used again for the return delivery. It is the same bag on the way in and the way out, so there is no risk of a gown being transferred to a different cover mid-process.

Wrapped and boxed small leather goods

Wallets, belts, buckles, watch straps and small leather goods travel in a padded box or a protective sleeve on the route. Shoes travel in pairs in a padded bag. Bags travel in a dust cover where the client provides one, or a soft wrap where they do not. Nothing rattles around loose in a rider's bag.

Photo tagging on collection

Every item is photographed on collection, and that photo is logged against your WhatsApp thread the same day. The photo is the record of what we received and the reference we compare against if there is any question later. It also protects both sides: the client knows the item was received exactly as they handed it over, and we know exactly what state the item arrived in.

Per-item tracking in the workshop

Each item carries a tag with your name, the booking reference and the service booked. That tag stays with the item from collection through the workshop and back to delivery. If a household sends five items on one pickup, the five items travel separately through the workshop with their own tags and are reassembled at delivery. Nothing gets mixed up with another client's items even when the same work is being done on several pieces at once.

Rider care

Our riders are not couriers in the abstract sense. They are the same people who handle our pickups every day, and they are trained to handle garments, leather goods and shoes with the same care the workshop would apply. A jacket is never folded onto itself in a way that creases the shoulder. A pair of shoes is never stacked sole to upper. A dress is never bundled. Small habits, but they are the reason we almost never have a transit issue.

If you have a specific concern: Tell us on WhatsApp before the pickup. If you want the item collected in a particular way, if it needs to travel flat rather than hung, if it needs to travel covered rather than exposed, we will note it on the collection instruction and the rider will follow it. No request is too specific where it protects the item.

What Is Not Covered by Free Pickup

Almost everything is covered. There are three honest exceptions, and it is better to say so upfront than to leave a client guessing.

Outside the Nairobi ring

Free pickup and delivery applies within the Nairobi ring. Anywhere beyond that — Mombasa, Nakuru, Kisumu, Eldoret, Nyeri, Thika, Machakos, Kajiado and any other town — is handled by courier at the client's cost. You send the item to our Industrial Area workshop using a courier of your choice, we repair it, and we ship it back using a courier of your choice at your cost. The same photo-first quote process applies: send photos on WhatsApp, get a firm price, then ship the item in.

Very large batches without advance notice

Batches over 40 items should be booked with advance notice so we can plan the route, the box space and the workshop queue. That said, there is no charge for a large batch — the pickup and delivery are still free — it is simply a matter of scheduling. Corporate clients with recurring monthly or quarterly pickups are scheduled a full year ahead.

Collection from a residence when no one is present and no drop-off arrangement has been made

If the rider arrives and no one is at the property and no gate or reception drop-off has been arranged, we cannot collect the item without a second trip. We do not charge for the second trip, but the pickup moves to the next available window. The way to avoid this is to leave the item with the gate, the reception desk or the house manager and tell us who to ask for by name.

Cancellation and Rescheduling

Cancellation and rescheduling are free at every stage. There is no cancellation fee, no rescheduling fee and no penalty for changing your mind.

Before collection

If you cancel before the rider is dispatched, there is no charge at all. If the rider has already left the workshop but has not yet reached your address, the same applies: no charge, no fee. The pickup simply moves to the next available window, or is removed from the schedule entirely.

After collection but before work begins

If you cancel after the item has been collected but before work has started, we return the item to you on a free delivery and there is no charge for either the collection or the return. Nothing is deducted for the rider time or the collection photos. This is the honest cost of running a free pickup service and we absorb it without passing it back to you.

After work has started

If you cancel after work has started, we stop where we are, return the item in its current state, and there is still no charge for the collection or the return. Where a partial job has been completed and the client wants to keep that partial result, we charge for the partial work at a fair rate agreed with the client before we continue. Where the client would rather have the item back untouched, we return it as it was collected.

After delivery

After the item has been delivered and paid for, cancellation is not possible because the work has been completed and accepted. Any further work on the same item is a new booking. Every completed job is covered by the 14-day workmanship warranty described on the warranty page.
