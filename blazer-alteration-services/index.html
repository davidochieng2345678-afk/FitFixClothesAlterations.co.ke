How We Alter Different Blazer Fabrics — A Tailor's Honest Guide

Not all blazers behave the same way during alteration. Here is a straight-talking guide to what we can and cannot do with each fabric type — written for anyone who wants to order expert blazer alterations with free door-to-door pickup in Nairobi and understand exactly what they are paying for.

Wool & Wool Blends

The most common blazer fabric — and the most forgiving to alter. Wool takes a pressed crease beautifully, which means slimmed waists and reset shoulders hold their shape permanently. Fine merino and Super 120s+ wools need lighter needle tension and careful seam allowances because the fibres are delicate. Standard tropical wool and wool-polyester blends are the easiest to work with and the most forgiving for a first-time alteration.

Linen & Linen Blends

Beautiful for Nairobi's warmer months but notoriously prone to fraying at the seams. Linen blazers require extra seam allowance in the original construction and careful overlocking during alteration. Sleeve shortening on linen is straightforward; waist take-in is trickier because linen does not press as crisply as wool, so the slimming must be done via re-shaped side seams rather than heat-formed darts. We handle linen daily in Nairobi.

Tweed & Heavy Wool

Tweed has a loose weave that can tear if you force the needle — this is the fabric that makes most Nairobi tailors refuse shoulder work. We use specialist hand-sewing techniques for tweed shoulder reduction and armhole resets, keeping the original woven structure intact. If you own a Harris, Donegal or Shetland tweed blazer that needs work, bring it to us — you have found the right workshop.

Cotton & Cotton Twill

Cotton blazers — often referred to as summer jackets or unstructured blazers — are typically unlined or half-lined, which makes them easier to alter in some ways and harder in others. Without canvas or padding, the alteration is more visible because there is no internal structure to hide the changes. We adjust these blazers conservatively, favouring small precise changes over large restructures.

Velvet, Silk & Evening Fabrics

Velvet and silk blazers are the hardest to alter without leaving visible marks. Both fabrics bruise under a needle, meaning every stitch leaves a small permanent indentation. This makes shoulder reduction and waist take-in extremely difficult — and means the tailor must have deep experience before touching the garment. David has altered silk-blend and velvet formal blazers throughout his career; we will advise honestly if a proposed alteration carries risk.

Synthetic & Poly-Blend Blazers

Polyester, viscose, and various synthetic blends are common in off-the-rack blazers and are generally easy to alter — with one major catch: heat damage. Poly blends melt under high iron temperatures, so pressing must be done through a protective cloth with carefully controlled heat. The seams also have less natural give, so alterations must be done with slightly looser allowances than on natural fibre. We handle synthetic blazers daily.

The Blazer Fit Guide — How It Should Actually Look on You

Before you book an alteration, it helps to know what a properly fitted blazer looks like. Here is the checklist David uses with every client — and the standard we hold every alteration to. Read it before you book a free pickup and you will know exactly what to ask for.

The Shoulders

The shoulder seam should end exactly at the point of your shoulder bone — no further. If the seam hangs past your shoulder and creates a divot below, the shoulders are too wide and need reduction. If the seam sits inside your shoulder line, the jacket is too small and generally cannot be expanded. This is the single most important measurement on the entire blazer, because shoulder width determines the entire drape of the jacket below it.

The Sleeves

The sleeve should end at the wrist bone, showing roughly 1 centimetre of shirt cuff when your arms are at rest. Any longer and the sleeve needs shortening. Any shorter and the sleeve cannot usually be lengthened more than a centimetre. Sleeve length is the easiest alteration and the one most Nairobi clients request first.

The Chest

When buttoned, the chest should feel smooth and close without pulling across the front. If the button pulls and creates an X-shaped wrinkle, the jacket is too tight. If the fabric billows away from the chest, the jacket is too loose. Chest width is altered via the side seams — never the front.

The Waist

The waist should follow the natural curve of your body — fitted but not cinched. You should be able to slip a hand under the jacket at the button when it is closed. Too tight and the pockets flare; too loose and the jacket loses its shape. This is the second most requested alteration after sleeve shortening.

The Length

A blazer should end at the middle of your seat, or slightly below the curve of your buttocks. Shorter blazers are more modern; longer blazers are more traditional. If your blazer ends too high above your seat, it can sometimes be lengthened — see our jacket lengthening services for details. If it hangs below the seat, it can be shortened, but this changes the proportion of the pockets and lower buttons.

The Lapels

Lapels should lie flat against your chest without curling or standing away. Notch lapels are classic and versatile; peak lapels are more formal. Narrowing or widening the lapel is possible but only within limits — usually 0.5cm either way before the alteration becomes visible.

The Vents

Side vents (the most common), centre vents, and ventless blazers all behave differently. The vent should lie flat when you stand still. If it opens or flares, the jacket is too tight across the hips. Vents can be adjusted slightly in length but are a subtle and risky alteration — best left to a trained tailor.

The Buttons

Buttons should be firmly attached and match the fabric's formality. Missing or loose buttons are the cheapest fix on the list — we replace buttons from KSh 200 each. If you want to get expert blazer button replacement and repair services near me Nairobi, this is a same-day job.

How to Care for Your Blazer So It Lasts Between Alterations

A well-cared-for blazer can last a decade or more without needing further alteration. Here are the care practices David teaches every client who collects a blazer from our workshop.

1. Brush It, Don't Wash It

A blazer should almost never be machine-washed — even the "dry clean" cycle wears the fabric and destroys the internal canvas over time. Instead, brush the jacket with a soft clothes brush after each wear to remove dust and surface dirt. This simple habit reduces dry-cleaning frequency by 50% or more and dramatically extends the life of the fabric.

2. Hang It Properly

Use a wide-shouldered wooden hanger that matches the width of your jacket's shoulders. Wire hangers distort the shoulder shape over time — which is exactly the area that is hardest and most expensive to alter. Give the blazer 24 hours to rest after each wear before hanging it back in the wardrobe.

3. Rotate Your Blazers

If you wear a blazer every day, own at least two and rotate them. Wool fibres need 24 hours to recover their natural elasticity between wears. Wearing the same blazer daily compresses the fabric permanently and makes it sag in the shoulders and elbows — reducing the number of alterations we can perform later.

4. Deal With Stains Immediately

Blot — never rub — a fresh stain with a clean white cloth. Take the blazer to a professional dry cleaner within 48 hours if the stain is oil-based or coloured. The longer a stain sits, the more it damages the fabric and the harder it becomes to remove without affecting the colour. Never use home stain removers on wool or silk.

5. Fix Small Issues Before They Become Big Ones

A loose button, a small tear, a fraying cuff — these are cheap and easy to fix when they are fresh. Left for months, they become structural problems requiring expensive repair. If you notice any of these, message us on WhatsApp — most small repairs are same-day and come with free pickup.

6. Store It Properly in the Off-Season

If you wear a blazer seasonally, dry-clean it before storage, place it in a breathable garment bag (never plastic), add cedar blocks or lavender sachets to deter moths, and store it flat or on a wide hanger in a dry cupboard. Never store a blazer folded — the shoulders crease permanently and the lapels lose their shape.

Follow these six habits and your blazer will hold its fit for years — meaning fewer alterations over its lifetime and more value from every jacket you own. If a repair is ever needed, we are one WhatsApp message away.

Blazer Alteration Questions Answered — Expert Advice from 14 Years of Tailoring

Real answers from owner David Ochieng, who has spent over a decade altering blazers at Manix Clothing Store, Italian Men's Wear and City Walk Ltd. If you have a question not answered here, send it on WhatsApp and David will reply personally.

Can a blazer be altered to fit perfectly?

Yes — almost any blazer can be altered to fit perfectly, within limits. Sleeves, waist, length, buttons and lining can all be adjusted. Shoulders are the most complex alteration because they determine the entire drape of the jacket, but with the right tailor (and the right technique), shoulders can be reduced or reshaped by 1–2 inches without ruining the jacket's structure. The only truly non-alterable elements are the overall fabric quantity (if the blazer is too small, we may not be able to let it out) and severe fabric damage.

How do I know if my blazer needs altering?

Signs your blazer needs altering: the sleeve ends past your wrist bone, the waist billows at the sides, shoulders extend past your natural shoulder bone, jacket length doesn't cover your seat properly, buttons feel loose or missing, the lining is torn, or you feel constricted when buttoning. Any of these means a professional alteration will transform how you look.

Can you alter a blazer's shoulders?

Yes — but it is the most complex blazer alteration and requires an experienced tailor. Shoulder alteration involves opening the jacket at the shoulder seam, reducing or reshaping the padding, adjusting the armhole, and re-stitching everything so the jacket maintains its structure. A badly done shoulder alteration is very obvious — the jacket will twist, pucker, or lose its shape. David Ochieng has performed hundreds of shoulder alterations over fourteen years. Blazer shoulder alteration cost in Kenya starts from KSh 2,500 — see our suit shoulder reduction page for details.

What is the difference between blazer alteration and bespoke tailoring?

Blazer alteration modifies an existing blazer — shortening sleeves, taking in the waist, adjusting shoulders, replacing buttons. Bespoke tailoring creates a new blazer from scratch, cut to your exact measurements. Alteration is faster (1–3 days) and more affordable; bespoke is a 3–4 week process and costs significantly more. For most men, alteration of an existing quality blazer is the best value — you keep the fabric and structure you already own while getting a perfect fit.

Yes. Can you shorten blazer sleeves with working buttonholes?

Yes. Working buttonholes (also called surgeon's cuffs) are a hallmark of quality tailoring and require a specific technique to shorten. We remove the sleeve at the shoulder, shorten from the top, and reattach — preserving every buttonhole exactly where it is. This is more complex than shortening a plain sleeve but delivers a flawless result and costs slightly more (from KSh 2,000).

Can you fix a blazer that's too tight, or add fabric back?

Sometimes — it depends on the seam allowance left in the original construction. Most quality blazers have 2–4 cm of hidden seam allowance in the side seams, which allows us to let out the waist by up to one size. If the blazer has been altered before and the allowance is gone, we cannot create fabric out of nothing, but we can often find clever workarounds (shifting buttons, adjusting the centre back). Send us a photo and we will tell you honestly whether it is possible.
